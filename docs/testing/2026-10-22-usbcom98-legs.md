# usbcom on Windows 98 SE under xHCI98 and NUSB's EHCI, 2026-10-22

The first guest runs of the 98 SE / ME driver ([first build](2026-10-22-usbcom98.md),
[design](../design/serial.md) route B). These are the 98 SE legs that the
owner's rule asks for (`AGENTS.md`): one under NUSB 3.3's EHCI driver and one
under xHCI98. Every leg is QEMU (TCG) with this package's simulated CDC-ACM
device, so each one is a VM pass, never a "Tested" cell. No physical serial
board was used. Nothing is released and no README "Tested by the author"
cell changes.

The legs found six driver faults. They are fixed in `3eefffe`
(section "Driver faults found"). With the fixes, every check passes on both
stacks, except a 64 KiB echo under NUSB's EHCI. That echo fails in about one
run in six because a block is duplicated or lost below the driver
("Below the driver: NUSB's EHCI under QEMU"). Under xHCI98, the free build's
opens failed with error 31 after some 55 to 65 opens. The simulator caused
that, not the driver. It is fixed in `6aa3cae` and the commit after it
("Free build under xHCI98").

## Setup

Kit `E:\usb-wdm-free\usbcom9x\`: scripts under `scripts\`, ports 48860-48869.
One guest ran at a time: headless `-machine pc -accel tcg -cpu pentium3,-apic
-smp 1 -m 256`, `-action reboot=shutdown`, with no NIC. Overlays sit on copies
of the net9x kit's bases:

| Guest | Base | Device bus |
|---|---|---|
| `x` | 98 SE with xHCI98 3.1.0.0 (`qemu-xhci,p2=4,p3=4`) | `xh.0` |
| `n` | the same plus NUSB 3.3; `usb-ehci` added, its driver installed from NUSB's `USB2.INF` at first boot | `eh.0` |

- **The device.** `acmemu.py` runs on 127.0.0.1:48862, attached with
  `usb-redir`. These model options are new for these legs:
  - `--speed high`: 512-byte bulk, a device qualifier and an other-speed
    configuration.
  - `--mirror`: DTR drives DSR, RTS drives DCD, and a sent break comes back
    once as `bBreak`.
  - `--profile pico`: device class EF/02/01, an IAD over the CDC pair, and a
    vendor interface.
  - 64-bit packet ids and 32-bit bulk lengths. Without them QEMU refuses the
    device on an xHCI port: "usb-redir-host lacks capabilities needed for use
    with XHCI".
- **The test program.** `COMTEST.EXE` (`comtest.c`) runs from `A:` through
  Win+R. It logs to the console, to `A:\COMTEST.TXT` and to port 0xE9. In
  the trace flavour (`build.cmd w98 trace`), `USBCOM.SYS` writes `uc:` lines
  and `USBCOMV.VXD` writes `uv:` lines into the same 0xE9 log.
- **`COMTEST COMn full`: 58 checks.**
  - Open, and the default DCB.
  - Echo of 1, 63, 64, 65, 128, 511, 512, 513, 1537 and 4096 bytes, then
    16384 and 65536 bytes. 65536 bytes is more than the 4096-byte receive
    queue holds, so the echo has to wait in the device.
  - 11 baud rates from 300 to 1000000, each with a 200- and a 1537-byte echo.
  - Eight framings: 7E1, 8O1, 8N2, 7M1, 7S1.5, 6N1, 5N1.5 and 8N1.
  - Seven `COMMTIMEOUTS` cases.
  - DTR and RTS as seen through DSR and DCD, in eight steps, with CTS always
    on.
  - `GetCommProperties`.
  - `SetupComm`, followed by echoes.
  - `PurgeComm` RX: data that was waiting is dropped and no stale bytes
    follow. `PurgeComm` TX.
  - `TransmitCommChar`.
  - `SetCommBreak` and `ClearCommBreak`.
  - A 30000-byte echo from one write.
  - The events `EV_DSR`, `EV_RLSD`, `EV_BREAK`, `EV_RXCHAR` and `EV_TXEMPTY`,
    on an overlapped handle.
  - A second open of the port is refused.
  - Open, echo and close, five times.
- **`COMTEST COMn hold 60 120`** is the pull while open (`pull.ps1`). Echoes
  run every 200 ms. The board is pulled after 15 s and plugged back 15 s
  later. When the hold time is up the dead handle is closed, the port is
  opened again, and the quick test runs on it.
- **Host load.** CPU was near 97 %, from 13 or more QEMU guests of other
  work. The timings below are noisy, and QEMU's `usb-redir` does not model
  bus time, so the bytes per second say nothing about real USB.

## Driver faults found (fixed in `3eefffe`)

1. **The VxD and the SYS had the same module name.** NTKERN lists
   `USBCOM.SYS` with VXDLDR as `USBCOM`. VCOMM's load of `USBCOM.VXD`
   therefore found the SYS's entry and sent it `Sys_Dynamic_Device_Reinit`
   (EAX 0x35, read from QEMU's exception log). The result was "A fatal
   exception 0E has occurred at 0028:53F000FF" on every open: a jump through
   linear address 1, into the real-mode interrupt table. A load through Win32
   `CreateFile` worked, but only because the SYS was not loaded yet. The VxD
   is now `USBCOMV.VXD`.
2. **VCOMM keeps a pointer into an unloaded port driver.** VCOMM unloads the
   port driver at the last close. It keeps the `PortName` pointer given to
   `_VCOMM_Add_Port` and compares against it at the next open. After a
   reload at another address, VCOMM faulted at VCOMM+53A, with EDI equal to
   CR2 and pointing into the old load. The VxD now refuses
   `Sys_Dynamic_Device_Exit` and stays loaded.
3. **`DriverControl` with `hDevNode` 0.** That call comes from a load other
   than VCOMM's. It used to add a port with a garbage name; it now adds
   none.
4. **The child never started.** 98 SE sends the `USBCOM\COMPORT` child no
   `IRP_MN_START_DEVICE`, because its loader is VCOMM. When its driver is
   installed, it gets `IRP_MN_REMOVE_DEVICE`. Every open's `Attach` was
   refused. The ports now run from the FDO's start.
5. **`UcAppendAscii` cut strings at 15 characters.** The FriendlyName came
   out as "USB Serial PortCOM4)". The Enum scan's self-exclusion path also
   lost its trailing backslash, so a board's own old `PortName` counted as
   used and the port moved from COM3 to COM4.
6. **VCOMM's Win32 `WriteFile` calls `PortWrite` once.** It then only waits
   for the queue to drain, and reports whatever was not taken as a failed
   write. Before the fix, a 16384-byte write wrote 4096 bytes. Microsoft's
   `serial.vxd` on COM1 took the same write whole, in 7 ms. The VxD's own
   transmit queue is now at least 64 KiB. Setting `CE_TXFULL` on a partial
   accept was tried, and VCOMM ignores it.

Two more crashes in the logs were not in the driver:

- One came from the guest's history: an earlier install's cached
  `USBCOMP.INF` still named `USBCOM.VXD`.
- One was a shutdown caused by the test harness: keystrokes went to the
  Start menu and chose Shut Down. The harness now uses Win+R.

## Settled on 98 SE (design 5.1.4, 14, 15.1)

- **`PortName`.**
  - `IoOpenDeviceRegistryKey(PLUGPLAY_REGKEY_DEVICE)` on the child PDO
    writes its `Enum` hardware key.
  - The write lands during the child's first `QUERY_ID`, before VCOMM loads
    the devnode.
  - VCOMM opens the port by that name.
  - The Enum scan reads `HKLM\Enum` through `ZwOpenKey` and
    `ZwEnumerateKey`.
  - A replugged board gets its remembered COM number back.
- **`_NtKernCreateFile`** opens `\Device\UsbComCOMn`, and the private IOCTL
  returns the table.
- **`DriverControl`** takes `(DriverFunction, hDevNode, RefData, AllocBase,
  AllocIrq, PortName)`.
- **`PDVersion` 0x0100** is accepted.
- **`Contention=*vcd`** works on a port without resources. It was also run
  with no `Contention` value (x5, x6), with the same result.
- **Removal.**
  - NTKERN sends the FDO `IRP_MN_REMOVE_DEVICE` with no `STOP` first.
  - The open port gets `Gone`. Echoes fail, and the modem status drops to
    CTS only.
  - The handle stays until the program closes it.
  - The port drains in 1 round of 50 ms under xHCI98 and in 34 rounds under
    NUSB.
  - The replugged device opens again within 50 ms of the close.
- **Composites.** xHCI98 splits the Pico model and binds `&MI_00`. NUSB 3.3
  presents it whole, so the whole-device id binds.
- **Install.** 98 SE runs the Add New Hardware wizard a second time for the
  child. It finds `USBCOMP.INF` in the same folder, but needs the user to
  click through. Once the INFs are in the cache, a second board installs
  silently.

## Below the driver: NUSB's EHCI under QEMU

The 64 KiB echo under NUSB's EHCI fails now and then. Over the eight-run
series it failed 2 of 8 times with the Pico model and 1 of 8 with the Uno
model, and it failed twice in the Pico model's `full` runs. Under xHCI98 it
never failed: three `full` runs and the free-build control series.

There were two kinds of failure:

- **A duplicated OUT block.** The block at offset 4096 reached the model
  twice. (This said "under two usbredir packet ids, with no cancel in
  between". The closer reading of 2026-10-23 found the same id both times,
  with a late cancel between.)
- **A lost IN block.** The model's log shows every block sent, but one
  4096-byte block never reached the program. QEMU had combined that block
  into a 16-28 KiB IN request.

`USBCOM.SYS` logged no transfer error, no reset and no resubmission in any
of these runs. The closer reading of 2026-10-23
([usbcom-nusb-echo](2026-10-23-usbcom-nusb-echo.md)) settles where this
happens. QEMU's EHCI model cancels a queue and replays it when NUSB's driver
updates an active qTD ("guest updated active qTD"). The replay sends an OUT
the device had already taken a second time, or loses an IN. `USBCOM.SYS`
now keeps one OUT transfer in flight, and 50 echoes passed with that. A
real controller with NUSB stays a REAL CHECK.

The same environment also explained two failures that the simulator has now
fixed (`fa456fe`, `58453e7`):

- **Floods of repeated notifications.** QEMU's EHCI model stops and restarts
  interrupt receiving whenever it retires an idle queue. The old model
  answered every restart with a SERIAL_STATE: about 600 in one event test.
- **Lost notifications.** QEMU discards interrupt packets it holds when
  receiving stops, which lost the looped-back break and once a DTR/RTS
  state. The model now holds notifications until the host is receiving, and
  after a restart it repeats the last state once.

## Free build under xHCI98

With the free build, `full` and the pull while open passed (x9). The longer
sequence was `full`, the pull, `opens 40`, then 64 KiB echoes one after
another. In it, every open from some point on failed with Win32 error 31
(x9, x13, x15: after about 55 to 65 opens). The trace build showed the same
failure (x17, `evidence\x-xhci-e9-x17.log`):

- The FDO and the child got `IRP_MN_REMOVE_DEVICE` (PnP 02) between two
  runs. Nothing had pulled the board.
- After that, the VxD's `_NtKernCreateFile` of `\Device\UsbComCOM4`
  returned 0xC0000034 (no such object). `PortOpen` therefore found no table
  and returned `IE_HARDWARE`, which VCOMM reported as error 31.
- At that moment the simulator had exited with "invalid/too many bulk IN
  requests". That exception ended the replugged board's simulator in every
  failing run (`tmp\x9-free-replug.err`, and the same file for x13, x15 and
  x17). The usbredir connection dropped, so QEMU removed the device.

The simulator now records the requests it refuses. That record, from x17's
second plug, gives the cause. At a close, `USBCOM.SYS` cancels its IN
transfers, and xHCI98 cancels each one in one of two ways:

- A cancelled TD at the ring's head is skipped with Set TR Dequeue.
- A cancelled TD behind a live one is rewritten in place as a No Op TRB.
  This is the cancel path in `hcd_cfg.c` that calls `XhciRingNoOpAt`, and it
  is a valid way to cancel a TD on an xHCI ring.

QEMU's `qemu-xhci` stops the endpoint and cancels all its packets at the
device. After the restart it submits the TDs again, and it passed the No Op
TD to the device as a bulk IN request of length 0. The simulator raised on
the zero length. A real controller completes a No Op TRB without a bus
transaction. The fault is therefore in QEMU and the simulator, not in xHCI98
or the driver.

The simulator now accepts a zero-length IN request. It completes the request
with no data once it is the oldest one pending. The first version of the
fix completed it at once, ahead of older requests, and in x19 that aborted
QEMU itself (`usb_packet_complete_one: assertion failed`,
`evidence\qemu-x19-abort.out`): QEMU completes an endpoint's packets only in
order. Host tests in `acmtest.py` cover both cases.

With the fixed simulator, x18 (free) and x20 (trace) passed every check in
the table above. The zero-length request did not occur in either run. It
depends on how the cancels race QEMU's restart: it occurred in two of four
trace runs (x17 and x19). The in-order completion is therefore covered by the
host test, but not yet by a guest run.

One change was reverted. It kept each port's queues between opens, and it
was made while error 31 looked like heap exhaustion in the VxD. The cause was
not in the VxD.

## The zero-length IN, made to happen at a close (split flavour)

The simulator's in-order completion of a zero-length IN had occurred in no
guest run after the fix. A test-only build flavour makes it happen: `split`
(`build.cmd w98 split`) is the trace flavour plus `UC_TEST_SPLIT_CANCEL`
(`ucio.c`, `UcSplitCancel`). It is never released, and the free and trace
builds have no code from it. At a close, before the other transfers, the
SYS:

1. cancels the third-oldest busy bulk IN alone, then waits for it and
   50 ms more;
2. cancels the oldest one alone, and waits the same way.

The oldest INs are the TDs on xHCI98's ring; xHCI98 queues the newer ones in
software. Step 1 is therefore xHCI98's in-place No Op rewrite of a TD behind
live ones. Step 2 cancels the ring's head, so xHCI98 moves the dequeue pointer
to the next live TD, which lies before the No Op. QEMU then fetches that TD
and the No Op after it, and sends the No Op to the device as a zero-length
bulk IN.

`COMTEST COMn closes N BYTES` opens the port, writes BYTES without reading
them back and closes at once, N times. It then opens the port again, lets
the rest of the echo arrive, drops it, and echoes 4096 bytes. In x25 (98 SE)
and m4 (ME, Pico model), every close gave the simulator one zero-length
request behind a live one: 11 and 61 of them. When QEMU cancelled the older
request, the simulator completed the zero-length one with no data. It now
runs its in-order completion after every cancel. QEMU did not abort, and
every check passed.

Two earlier attempts did not produce the request, and they show how QEMU
behaves:

- **Cancelling the newest IN** (x21) touched only a transfer that xHCI98
  still held in software.
- **Cancelling the second-oldest alone** (x22 to x24) did rewrite a TD as a
  No Op. QEMU's `qemu-xhci` then resumed the endpoint at its own fetch
  pointer, past the ring's live TDs. The live INs before the No Op were never
  fetched again: in x24 they stayed busy until the close cancelled them. A
  real xHCI controller resumes at the stopped TD. On QEMU, though, a driver
  that cancels one of several queued reads under xHCI98 leaves the others
  waiting. This is recorded for xHCI98 as a QEMU-only behaviour. It does not
  touch usbcom, which cancels a pipe's transfers only all together.

## ME under NUSB's EHCI: not possible

NUSB 3.3's and 3.6's installers (`tools\nusb33e.exe`, `nusb36e.exe` in
xHCI98's tree, read as files and not run) both carry the text "Only for
Windows 98SE English !!!". There is no ME leg under NUSB. ME's USB 2.0 legs
are xHCI98's only.
