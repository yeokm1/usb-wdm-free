# uvc98 on 98 SE with the simulated camera: NUSB's EHCI and xHCI98, 2026-10-23

The 98 SE legs of `uvc98` under NUSB's EHCI could not pass with the Brio
100 passed through: QEMU's `usb-host` restarted the camera's isochronous
stream 275 times in one capture (`2026-10-22-98se-nusb-legs.md`). This
record runs the same legs with `uvcsim.py` (`src/video/uvc98/`), a
SIMULATED UVC camera over `usb-redir` that sends one packet per
(micro)frame in real time. That removes the host passthrough from the
path. Development legs, not a release and not "Tested".

## NUSB 3.3 EHCI (QEMU `usb-ehci`): FAIL in QEMU

Driver counters per run (from the 0xE9 trace; "sent" is the simulator's
log for the same stream):

| Run | Size, alternate | Sent | Guest received | Zero-length | Failed | FID flips / EOFs | Frames delivered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default `maxframes` | 160x120, alt 1 (192) | 55595 in 6.95 s | 37248 (67 %) | 11554 | 766 | 128 / 1 | 0 (102 bad, 26 size) |
| default | 320x240, alt 3 (640) | 56980 in 7.1 s | 56416 (99 %) | 15022 | 1010 | 120 / 2 | 0 (111 bad, 8 size) |
| default | 640x480, alt 7 (3 x 800) | - | - | - | - | - | guest hung after State 3 |
| `maxframes=512` | 160x120, alt 1 | 57335 | 39216 (68 %) | 17430 | 1010 | 103 / 2 | 0 (89 bad, 14 size) |
| `maxframes=512` | 320x240, alt 3 | - | - | - | - | 118 / 1 | 0 (103 bad, 14 size) |
| `maxframes=512`, `--fps 5` | 640x480 at 5 fps, alt 3 (640) | 53859 | 36104 (67 %) | 8697 | 615 | 22 / 0 | 0 (21 bad) |

Every READ ended in error 1784 (one, under QEMU tracing, in 1460).

- Every zero-length packet the driver counted is a QEMU underrun: the
  simulator sends at least a 12-byte header in every microframe, and QEMU's
  `usb-redir` answers an empty buffer with a zero-length success.
- NUSB saw no schedule gaps (`USB_GAPS` 0: every URB started where the last
  ended), and QEMU's EHCI frame clock ran at wall-clock rate (20 frame-list
  rollovers in 20.6 s idle, 83 in about 80 s while streaming).
- The 640x480 hang at 30 fps is high-bandwidth specific: at 5 fps the same
  size on a single-transaction alternate streams without hanging. It
  matches the passthrough leg's 640x480 hang.
- Control on the same QEMU binary, guest and simulator: under xHCI98 the
  160x120 stream had 206 zero-length and 0 failed packets, and passed
  (below). So the simulator's stream and uvc98's payload path are sound,
  and the loss is specific to NUSB on QEMU's EHCI.

Verdict: **FAIL in QEMU at every size**, now with the passthrough removed.
The control below, Windows 2000's own `usbehci.sys` on the same QEMU
`usb-ehci` with the same simulator and driver build, passes every size. So
QEMU's EHCI with `usb-redir` carries this stream, and the loss is in how
NUSB drives it.

| Run | Result | Readings |
|---|---|---|
| 160x120, alt 1 (192) | **PASS** | three frames, byte-exact copies of the simulator's pictures 1, 3 and 5; 7 failed packets; stop selected alternate 0 |
| 320x240, alt 3 (640) | **PASS** | three complete changing frames, 5 dropped; failed packets 18 (cumulative) |
| 640x480 at 30 fps, alt 7 (3 x 800, high bandwidth) | **PASS** | three complete changing frames, 4 dropped; failed packets 23 (cumulative); `usbehci.sys` accepts uvc98's back-to-back high-bandwidth packets (`a8bf2bc`) |

Zero-length packets (QEMU underruns) are as common here as under NUSB, and
they do not stop frames. The difference is the failed packets. 2000 saw 7
to 23 over all three runs; NUSB saw 766 to 1010 in every run, about 140 a
second, so nearly every 33 ms frame lost a payload and was dropped as bad.
QEMU printed no babble or "unexpected iso usb result" message in either
guest, so these failures take a silent path. Which one is not read: a
transaction NUSB scheduled too close to the current frame for the emulated
controller, or a device or endpoint lookup that fails. Either way the same
emulated controller serves 2000's driver without them.

This is also the first 2000 `usbehci.sys` run of a high-bandwidth
isochronous stream in this project: in QEMU, with the simulator, it
carries one (`2026-10-22-uvc-m2.md` left it unverified because the host
passthrough failed first).

## xHCI98 3.1.0.0 (QEMU `qemu-xhci`)

| Run | Result | Readings |
|---|---|---|
| 160x120, alt 1 | **PASS** | three frames, byte-exact copies of the simulator's pictures 1, 4 and 44; 0 failed packets, 206 zero-length; stop selected alternate 0 |
| 320x240, alt 3 (640) | **PASS** | three complete changing frames, 8 dropped (7 bad, 1 size) |
| 640x480, 30 fps, alt 7 (3 x 800) | **FAIL, driver defect** | every isochronous URB refused: `USB_FIRSTFAIL C000000D`, USBD `80000300` (INVALID_PARAMETER), 13 submissions; no iso stream ever started on QEMU's side |
| 640x480, 5 fps, alt 3 (640) | **FAIL in QEMU** | 50421 packets sent in 6.3 s, 10552 received (21 %), 1319 URBs instead of about 6300, 2 gaps; all 18 frames dropped for size |
| unplug during a 160x120 capture (`--unplug-after 2`) | **PASS** | READ 1-2 good, READ 3 error 1784; `UVU_STOP`, `USB_CLOSED`; guest alive. That first run also replugged and unplugged seven times by a simulator bug (fixed, below); the guest survived all seven |
| replug (`--replug-after 8`), then 160x120 | **PASS** | `disconnect_ack`, driver reloaded (`M1_DRIVER_ENTRY`, `USB_OPEN 0`), three complete changing frames |

**The 640x480 refusal is uvc98's.** For High-Speed high-bandwidth
alternates `UvcIsoPlan` lays packets 4096 bytes apart (design section 5.4,
one packet per page). USBD gives an isochronous packet no length of its
own: its length is the distance to the next packet's offset.
xHCI98's `XhciPipeIsoCheck` (`src/xhci_pipe.c`) therefore refuses a
4096-byte packet on an endpoint whose payload is 2400 bytes. The real
Brio's 640x480 (3 x 1020) would meet the same refusal. The fix belongs to
uvc98: lay packets at their capacity, accepting the second TRB a
page-crossing packet costs. Done in `a8bf2bc` (owner decision
2026-10-23); see "After the stride fix".

**320x240 and up at the full rate is limited by QEMU's xHCI.** The 5 fps
control shows the guest taking about a fifth of the microframes. xHCI98
saw a contiguous schedule (2 gaps in 1319 URBs), so the shortfall is in
how fast `qemu-xhci` runs isochronous TDs. This is 43.60's finding: SIA TDs
are paced one per timer kick with no catch-up. QEMU's `usb-redir` drops
what does not fit its buffer, and keeps runs of up to about 960 packets.
That is why 160x120 and 320x240 frames (214 and 245 packets) still get
through, and 640x480 frames (about 1600 packets at 5 fps) never do.
The 320x240 pass here is therefore a pass of the driver path, not of a
sustained 30 fps stream: 3 frames were delivered and 8 were dropped.

## After the stride fix (`a8bf2bc`), xHCI98

`uvcm2.sys` checked VM-trace build `A174DB0F...C758` (`isotest` 2486348
checks PASS), copied into the same overlay with `D2.BAT` before the plug.

| Run | Result | Readings |
|---|---|---|
| 640x480, 30 fps, alt 7 (3 x 800) | **PASS** | xHCI98 accepts every URB (508 submitted, `USB_FIRSTFAIL` 0); three frames, byte-exact copies of the simulator's pictures 1, 17 and 68; 14 dropped (11 bad, 3 size) |
| 160x120 (regression) | **PASS** | three complete changing frames; the one saved in time is byte-exact |

What QEMU's pacing allows: the simulator sent pictures 1 to 68 in 2.23 s. In
that time the guest took 3216 packets, about 1440 a second, or 18 % of the
microframes: about 3.4 MB/s of the 18.4 MB/s sent. So about 1.3 complete
frames a second got through. A 640x480 frame at 30 fps is 258 packets of
2388 bytes, short enough to survive inside one of `usb-redir`'s unbroken
runs. At 5 fps the same frame spans 1600 microframes, and none survives.
The pass therefore shows that xHCI98 accepts and carries uvc98's
high-bandwidth URBs. It says nothing of a sustained 30 fps, which QEMU's
xHCI cannot carry.

## Simulator fixes found by these legs

- On a SuperSpeed-capable `qemu-xhci` port, QEMU refused the device
  ("usb-redir-host lacks capabilities needed for use with XHCI") until the
  simulator offered `64bits_ids` and `32bits_bulk_length` as well as
  `ep_info_max_packet_size` (`6f6f1b3`).
- A timed unplug re-fired after every replug (fixed with a host test, this
  record's commit).

## Real checks needed

1. **uvc98 on 98 SE under NUSB 3.3's EHCI on a real EHCI controller**, a
   High-Speed UVC camera (the Brio 100) on an EHCI port, 160x120 and
   320x240 YUY2 with `M1CAP`. Look for three complete changing frames and
   the driver's failed and zero-length packet counts near 0 (the 0xE9
   trace is VM-only; the frames are the check). The 2000 control puts the
   QEMU loss on NUSB's side. This check decides whether NUSB loses
   payloads on real hardware too, or only against the emulated controller
   under TCG timing.
2. **uvc98 on 98 SE under xHCI98 on real xHCI hardware**, 320x240 and
   640x480 with the `a8bf2bc` build or later. Look for a sustained frame
   rate near 30 fps, not three frames.
