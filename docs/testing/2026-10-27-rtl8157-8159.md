# rtl815x: the RTL8157 and RTL8159 in the chip core, 2026-10-27

Task 44.70 part B2 (owner, 2026-10-27, xHCI98 3.3.0.0): the RTL8157
(chip id `1030`, 5 Gb/s) and RTL8159 (`2020`, 10 Gb/s) in `rtl815x`'s
chip core (`ethernet.md` section 6.4.1). Not a release, not "Tested": no
such adapter exists here, and the owner accepted host tests and the
Python simulator only. No VM leg was run for these chips.

## What was done

- Linux `drivers/net/usb/r8152.c` at v7.3 (the file `ethernet.md` pins at
  `f47d2236`) read for facts only; the chips' sequences restated as step
  tables. Commits on branch `rtl-5g10g`, on the integrator's 8156-core
  hooks (`rtl-new` `3084ca6`): `a032e28` (the chips), `57b83ed` (review
  fixes).
- Firmware: Linux names `rtl8157-1.fw` and `rtl8159-1.fw` but brings both
  chips up without them (applying a patch returns at once when none was
  loaded). The core loads none. Reported by the simulator's author (Linux
  commit `a51e171e`, not read here): an RTL8159 without its patch links
  at 5 Gb/s at most.

## Independent second reading

Before the integrator's shared tables landed, the two chips were written
once on their own (`d2682f7`, not merged: superseded): every step of
Linux's setup, PHY setup, up, down, enable, disable and unload for
`RTL_VER_16/17`. A one-off harness (scratch, not kept) linked that core
and the merged one (`a032e28`) to `rtltest.c`'s register model and wrote
every control request, sleep and result over whole lives (Start, filters,
link at 1000, 100 half, 10 full, SetMac, down, 10 half, Suspend, Resume,
Stop) for both chips at Full, High and SuperSpeed, in 13 model variants
(plain; PHY in external init or powered down; power-cut flag; efuse;
saw counter; link up at start; 32-pin package; burst size; patch window
refused; external init with its flags; flash loading; power cut with the
restore done) and with a failure injected at every third request.

Result: 36 of 78 lives byte-identical; every difference is one of three
known choices: the RTL8159's bulk-IN buffer (48 KiB in the first, 32 KiB
in the merged core, so the early receive size differs), a refused patch
window (the first left out the following PHY reset, as Linux does; the
merged core resets, as for the RTL8156B), and the RTL8159's restore wait
(21 reads against 20).

## Other chips unchanged

The same harness over every other id (4C00 to 6400, 7020 to 7420, and the
refused 7010) at three USB speeds and 13 variants: against `3084ca6`,
624 of 624 lives identical after `a032e28`. After `57b83ed` (the EEE guard
fix) the 8152, 8153, 8153B/C and 7020 lives stay identical; the 7030,
7400, 7410 and 7420 each lose two duplicate EEE steps (an EEE enable and
an early advertisement), now in Linux's order.

## Review

Codex (static review of `d2682f7` against `r8152.c`): no P1; the tuning
tables matched entry by entry. P2 findings acted on: the EEE guard count
(a shared-table bug, `57b83ed`), the EEE+ vector, wait counts and sleeps
checked, every failure point. Left as is: the count-based waits (10 reads
2 ms apart for the indirect window, 20 reads 1 ms apart for the restore)
stand in for Linux's 20 ms deadlines; their sleeps alone reach the
deadline and each read adds USB time, so they never give up sooner.
A second review (the merged `a032e28` and `57b83ed`): no P1 or P2; the
merged paths, hooks, indirect window, EEE and speed advertisement and
DESC_V2 agree with `r8152.c`, and the guard fix is right for every chip.
Its P3s: clear-bit checks now seed the bits (`4f5b7ed`); the receive
truncation loops still check only that a rejected transfer emits
nothing, as the first format's do.

REAL CHECK NEEDED: an RTL8157 and an RTL8159 adapter, at USB 3 and USB 2,
on each target system: the trace's "chip start" and link speed, DHCP,
`ping -l 1472`, cable out and in, removal; the RTL8159's top speed
without its PHY patch.
