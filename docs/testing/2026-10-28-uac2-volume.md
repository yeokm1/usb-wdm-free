# uac2 volume and mute (98 SE, XP x86, XP x64), 2026-10-28

A user reported that `uac2` plays but offers no volume control: "If I
didn't have like a passive audio mixer it would be blowing out my
eardrums." They use Windows XP x64 and a CX31993-based headphone DAC.
The owner put playback volume and mute into release 0.1.4. This record
runs them with simulated devices on 98 SE, XP x86 and XP x64.
Development legs in QEMU, not "Tested". Branch `uac2-vol` from `48c1366`
(release 0.1.4).

## What uac2 0.1.4.0 does

- **Controls.** The topology filter's speaker line carries a volume node
  and a mute node after its SUM node (`uactopo.c`). WDMAud shows them as
  the Speaker line's "Master Volume" and "Master Mute". Windows Vista and
  later find them on the path to the speaker pin. A capture-only device's
  stub line has none.
- **The device's controls.** `uacvol.c` walks back from each Output
  Terminal to the playback USB streaming terminal. On that path it takes
  the Feature Unit nearest the output with a read/write master volume
  (mute). Failing that, it takes one with the control read/write on every
  logical channel. The driver reads each control's RANGE and CUR. It
  writes SET_CUR only to controls the descriptor marks read/write.
- **Levels.** The node has two channels (one on a mono device). Windows
  sets them in 1/65536 dB. The driver turns them into the device's steps
  (1/256 dB) and leaves any rest to a software gain on the samples on
  their way to USB. A master volume plays the louder channel. The other
  channel's balance is done in software. Channel controls each play their
  own channel.
- **No hardware control.** Without a writable volume (mute) the driver
  scales (zeroes) the samples itself, from -96 dB to 0 dB in 0.5 dB
  steps. KMixer's own software volume was not used. 98 SE's mixer shows
  no master volume for a device whose topology has no volume node.
- **Precautions.** The software gain follows what the device is known
  to do. An unknown setting counts as the top of its range. An extra
  channel volume whose RANGE cannot be read, and whose CUR is unknown,
  silences its channel. Lowering writes and mutes go first. A write that
  would raise a channel waits until the audio already queued has played,
  and is held when a lowering write or a mute has failed. Two limits
  remain (see "Left open" under "Review").
- **Who sets it.** Until Windows sets a control, the device keeps its own
  setting and the driver adds no software volume. From the first SET the
  driver owns that control. It stores the levels in the device's driver
  key (`Uac2Level`) and restores them at the next start. In master mode
  it then sets the same unit's channel volumes to 0 dB and unmutes its
  channel mutes, so the master alone sets the level.
- **IRQL.** SET_CUR runs at PASSIVE level. A property set at a raised
  IRQL is applied by one preallocated work item.
- **Diagnostics.** `Uac2VolMap` in the driver key: the units found, the
  modes, the range, the levels, the device's values, write counts.

Linux (`sound/usb` v6.12) has no quirk for the CX31993 (`0572`/`06cb`)
[linux]. Two of its generic rules are followed: a RES of 0 on a wider
range reads as 1, and a device that refuses a long RANGE request is asked
for one subrange [linux]. Linux marks `0572:1b08` and `0572:1b09` as
devices whose lowest volume step mutes [linux]. The driver needs no change
for that: at the lowest step the device is silent anyway.

The release images are built again from the release commit's sources,
which are the same; a build's PE timestamp differs.

`UACWAV` (`uacwav.c`) gained `mix` (the mixer of the first UAC2 output:
lines, controls, values), `vol`, `mute` and `ramp <s>`. `ramp` plays a
half-scale 440 Hz sine and steps the speaker line's volume through 65535,
32768, 16384 and 4096, then mute on and off, `<s>` seconds a step. It
restores what it found. These go through the mixer API, as the Volume
Control does.

`uacemu.py` gained `--fu full|master|channels|readonly|none`,
`--vol-range` and `--vol-cur`. It answers only the controls the
descriptor declares, takes a SET_CUR only on the range's steps (or the
silence value) and logs every SET. Every 0.25 s it logs the peak of each
channel as received and as rendered through the units then in force. A
level made in hardware moves the rendered peak only. A level made in the
driver moves the received peak. The `cx31993` profile (from the branch
`uac2-midi`) has a master mute and a volume on each channel, no master
volume.

## Found here

- **XP x64 and usbaudio.** XP x64 ranks Windows' signed `wdma_usb.inf`
  (`USB\Class_01`) above the unsigned `uac2wdm.inf`, even with
  `uac2wdm.inf` staged first: "USB Audio Device". Updating the device to
  `uac2` (`UpdateDriverForPlugAndPlayDevices`) then hung 2 of 2 here (`x1`,
  `x2`): `uac2` never started, and the shell stopped answering. The
  `uac2-midi` record saw the same and found `uac2` bound after a restart.
  The legs therefore moved `wdma_usb.inf` aside before the first plug, on
  XP x86 too (its ranking was not read).
- **98 SE, a hung guest (`s2`, the `4dd37d3` build).** After a cold boot
  with `hs24 --fu none` the startup sound played with the restored
  levels, balance included, in software. About 25 minutes later the guest
  showed a black text screen. Several of this rig's keystrokes went to it
  in that time. The fresh overlay `s3` with the final build ran the same
  device through two boots, the ramp and the Volume Control, and shut down
  cleanly. Not reproduced.
- **98 SE, `cx31993` first arrival.** The boot that installed it showed
  `waveOut` 0 (as the `uac2-midi` record found). The next cold boot played.
- **98 SE, long buffers.** A 12 s `UACWAV play` took 22 s of guest time
  and did not finish its eight buffers. A 2 s play finished in 8.5 s. This
  guest's clock runs slowly under TCG. `ramp` allows for it.

## Host tests

- `test.cmd`: `voltest.c`, 506989 checks: the walk on the recorded X4
  layout and built graphs (mixer, loops, truncation, read-only, reserved
  lanes), RANGE (silence, RES 0, split and non-divisible ranges), the dB
  conversion both ways for every 16-bit value, nearest-step snapping
  against a brute-force oracle, the gain table against `pow()`, targets and
  gains (master with extras, channel mode with its own ranges, mutes,
  silence, unknown state), sample scaling at 16/24/32 bits against a
  floating-point oracle, and the SET_CUR/GET_CUR/GET_RANGE setup bytes
  (`uacctl.c`). `topotest.c`: the volume and mute nodes on the render line,
  none on the capture-only stub.
- `uacsimtest.py`: 21 tests, including the driver's walk (`uacvol.c` in the
  host DLL) on every built profile and Feature Unit mode, the `cx31993`
  profile, RANGE answers through the driver's parser, the access rules and
  the rendered level.
- Builds: wdmx86 and wdm64, free and checked, with the import gate
  (`ZwQueryValueKey` added for the saved levels; it is in 98 SE's
  `wdm.lib`).

## Review

Five review rounds. Fixed in them: the work item's lifetime and one
item at a time; gains from the device's confirmed state; per-channel
RANGE; the silence value; ownership from the first SET; the unit's other
channel controls; per-channel mutes; the upper end of a range off its
step grid; size probes; CPU_RESOURCES; the simulator's step check and
rendered levels; removal and stop close the property sets; no software
volume before the volume is owned; lowering writes and mutes before
raising ones, and raising ones held after a failed one; an extra channel
volume without a RANGE still counted, and silenced while unknown; the
simulator's `cx31993` profile refuses `--fu`.

Left open from the last round:
- The 40 ms wait before a raising write covers the USB queue (at most
  32 ms) but not a packet the USB worker filled with the old gain and has
  not submitted yet. That packet plays at the new hardware level.
- In master mode a unit channel control that cannot reach 0 dB is
  counted in the gain (never too loud) but the master is not raised to
  make up for it, so that channel stays quieter than asked.

## Not run

- Windows 7 x86. The keyboard-only install through an elevated prompt did
  not complete in this rig. Vista and 7 x86 and x64, 2000, ME and 8 have no
  volume leg. On Vista and later the endpoint volume should find the
  node; not verified.
- NUSB's EHCI on 98 SE. A real device.
- A volume change made on the device itself (buttons): the driver reads no
  interrupt endpoint and sends no change event to Windows.
- Capture gain: not done.
