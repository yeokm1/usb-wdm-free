# uac2 playback garbled after seeking: cause, fix and legs, 2026-10-31

The open report of `docs/status.md` (a user on 32-bit XP with a Conexant
CX31993 DAC, `uac2` 0.1.5.0: playback starts clean, seeking can leave
the sound garbled; VLC with WAV and FLAC, Windows Media Player with
FLAC through K-Lite, not Windows Media Player with WAV). Read and fixed
on branch `w45-uacseek` from `6e378a9`. The cause and the change are
design record section 17 (`docs/design/uac2.md`). Development legs in
QEMU (TCG) with the simulator, not "Tested": no real CX31993 and no
player (VLC, Windows Media Player) was run.

## Rig

- Guests: XP SP3 x86, 2000 SP4 and 7 SP1 x86 as q35 overlays of the
  shared clean bases; 98 SE on the pc-machine pristine base (the q35 98 SE
  base does not boot on the stock QEMU that usb-redir needs). Stock QEMU
  11.1.0, `usb-redir` to the simulator.
- Host controllers: Windows' own `usbehci.sys` (`ich9-usb-ehci1`, no
  xHCI98); xHCI98 3.3.0.0 as released (`qemu-xhci`; the 3.4.0.0 package
  was not to hand at the time of the legs, and nothing of the cause or
  the fix is in the host controller driver).
- Device: `uacemu.py --profile cx31993` (High Speed, one synchronous
  playback endpoint, `bInterval` 1, 16/24/32-bit alternates, no
  feedback), its received PCM captured (`--pcm`, `--pcm-max 64`);
  `--rates 44100` where named, so KMixer opens the device at 44.1 kHz and
  the samples arrive unconverted.
- Drivers: `uac2` 0.1.5.0 as released (`uac2wdm.sys` `528f2ad0...`), and
  the fix, `1ab0dab` (`uac2wdm.sys` x86 `45f24830...`, x64 `4e125e64...`,
  built clean). Installed from `w2k-xp`'s files (the same binary and INF
  as `w98se-me`): on XP through `DevicePath` and `UACCI.INF` at the first
  plug, on 2000 likewise with a never-seen product id, on 7 through
  `pnputil -a` and `UACCI.INF`, on 98 SE through the Add New Hardware
  wizard (the KS files from the 98 SE CD).
- `UACSEEK.EXE` (`uacseek.c`, `seek.cmd`; `c22e6e5e...` for the legs
  after 03:40, `ab827c00...` before, the same but for the volume step)
  plays a counted signal and seeks three times, 2 s apart:
  `wo` WinMM with eight 20 ms buffers (`p`: pause, reset, new buffers,
  restart; `r`: reset only), `ds` DirectSound with a one-second looping
  buffer (`s`: Stop, SetCurrentPosition(0), refill, Play; `h`: Stop and
  Play only, no seek). `uacseek.py` reads the capture back (section 17.5
  of the design for the stale-block rule; `--coarse` for a stream KMixer
  or the audio engine resampled).

## What a seek is at the driver

On XP (both host controllers) and 7, the device's stream ran without a
break through every WinMM and DirectSound seek and pause tried: the
simulator saw the playback alternate selected once per test, so the
driver sees a seek only as a gap in the data written to its buffer. On
2000 the stream was stopped and started again at the seeks and pauses
(the alternate deselected and selected again each time; whether by
`KSSTATE_PAUSE` or `KSSTATE_STOP` was not read), with no stale block. No
98 SE stream ran (below).

Notes:

- Under xHCI98 on `qemu-xhci` the isochronous stream ran slower than
  real time in these TCG guests (about 2 s of audio in the 9 s a test
  took); each capture still holds all four runs of the signal (the three
  seeks).
- KMixer opened the device at 16 bits for the 24-bit client (`x24`): the
  24- and 32-bit alternates were not exercised. The change does not
  depend on the format; zero is silence for every PCM format `uac2`
  takes.
- With DirectSound, Stop then Play resumes at DirectSound's own play
  cursor: the first 20 to 30 ms after a resume repeat what was sent just
  before the stop, with either driver. That is DirectSound's, not a
  stale block.
- The prototype of the change without item 3 of design 17.4 (taken bytes
  not cleared) still read one stale block in 8 s of DirectSound pausing
  on XP (`p1-dsh48`); with item 3, none in any leg.

## 98 SE: owed

The same header runs on 98 SE, so the change applies there; its leg was
not read. On the pc-machine 98 SE guest with xHCI98 3.3.0.0, `uac2`
(the fix) installed through the Add New Hardware wizard, first with
some WDM audio files missing (Code 2, "NTKERN.VXD, MMDEVLDR.VXD device
loader(s) ... could not load the device driver"), then, with the KS and
WDM audio files taken from the 98 SE CD, "This device is working
properly". In the boots after that, with the simulator attached at
power-on (a plug during a session can hit 98 SE's documented first-plug
freeze), WinMM listed no `uac2` output and DirectSound no device, and
the device saw no stream: the guest's WDM audio stack, set up by hand,
was not brought to the state the earlier 98 SE legs had. Owed: the seek
legs on a 98 SE guest whose `uac2` plays (as the 2026-10-29/30 legs had).

## Two findings on 2000, not the change's

- **Changing the driver of a device that has Windows' USB Audio driver
  to `uac2` stops 2000.** Device Manager, Update Driver, Have Disk
  `uac2` on the "USB Audio Device" (`usbaudio`, Code 10) under Windows'
  own EHCI: STOP 0x0000001E (C0000005, F20C9D75, 0, 44)
  `KMODE_EXCEPTION_NOT_HANDLED` in `usbhub20.sys`, twice (the second with
  automatic restart off, so the screen was read). The stop left
  `ks.sys` corrupt: the next boot stopped with `c000026c` (`ks.sys`
  could not be loaded, 0xC0000221). Read further in
  `2026-10-31-uac2-2000-stop.md`: the stop is in `usbhub20.sys`, inside
  PortCls's `PcAddAdapterDevice`, after the copy; not `uac2`'s code. On XP the same
  change waits until a restart (`2026-10-29-uac2-xp-hang.md`). Installing
  `UACCI.INF` before the first plug avoids it: the first plug installs
  `uac2` and nothing is changed afterwards.
- On 2000 the first audio device of a system asks for a restart; the
  q35 2000 base's warm restart hangs in real mode (EIP 0x0D4F, its
  README): cold-start.

## Not covered

- A real CX31993, VLC and Windows Media Player: not run. Why Windows
  Media Player with WAV was spared was not read.
- 98 SE (above), XP x64, Vista, 8, 8.1, x64 builds and ME: not run. The change is in
  `uacstream.h`, one header for every system and both architectures.
- A ring set no larger than the URB queue (design 17.4 item 5).
