# uac2 0.1.8.0 on 98 SE, ME and 2000: update, regression and the CPU-load comparison, 2026-11-04/05

The sibling of `2026-11-04-uac2-queue.md` for the x86 binary on the 9x
systems and 2000 (worker uwf-audio9x; it finishes and replaces that
record's "98 SE (partial)" section). These are development legs in QEMU
(TCG) with the simulator, not "Tested": there was no real device and no
game. Raw logs, screenshots and scripts are in
`E:\xhci98-vm\p46\uwf-audio9x\` (`STATE.md` indexes them; `results9.txt`
and `r9-*.out` hold every run's numbers, the invalid ones included).

## Rig

- Guests: q35 overlays, 1 vCPU and 512 MB each, at most three at once, on
  the usb4-emu QEMU fork build `cb36497` (its isochronous real-time fix
  and `usb-redir`). 98 SE: an overlay of the earlier worker's 98 SE overlay
  (xHCI98 3.4.0.0 and `uac2` 0.1.6.0 already installed). ME and 2000:
  fresh overlays of the shared clean bases.
- xHCI98 3.4.0.0 as released, `INSTALL.BAT /console /yes /nopause`: ME
  exit 3, then a cold start; 2000 exit 3, the controller started at once (a
  second run: exit 0, "No changes were made").
- Device: `uacemu.py --profile cx31993` (High Speed, one synchronous
  playback endpoint, `bInterval` 1) through `usb-redir` on port 1;
  `--profile hs24` for record.
- Builds (all `wdmx86` free, 0.1.8.0 in the INF unless said): 0.1.6.0 (the
  `w98se-me` / `w2k-xp` release folders, `uac2wdm.sys` `dd5e14f5...`);
  HEAD `9f30c79` (the queue and the wake increment; `df1f7078...`, built at
  `1976c9e`); build 20 `2249164` (the real-time worker, `1de6931b...`);
  build 21 `7430bf2` (installed on 98 SE, not measured); build 22 `45f011a`
  (`b0a7a9bd...`); build 24 `899b9f4` (`388932b5...`). Build 23 was not
  run.
- Load: `BUSY.EXE`, one busy thread at normal priority, started minimized
  for the five plays. Each condition: an XHCITR report before and after
  (completion code 14, Ring Underrun, from the controller's counters),
  five `UACWAV play 2 16 48000 60` (WinMM), the driver key read back
  (`Uac2UsbStats`), the simulator's OUT packets and gaps between the
  host times of the plays' start and end.
- Paired runs. The host (32 threads) was shared with other workers' guests
  and was often about 70 % busy. Several runs on a single guest went at a
  third to a half of real time (60 s plays that ended after 27 to 44 s of
  guest time, Ring Underrun in the thousands) and are not used. A valid
  condition is a pair: a guest on 0.1.6.0 and its twin (an overlay of the
  same disk) on the build under test, playing at the same time, each taking
  320 to 360 s of host time for its five plays (the expected 5 x 60 s plus
  the batch's overhead).

- On 98 SE and ME a normal-priority busy thread hurts 0.1.6.0 far less
  than on XP x64 (where it fell to about 4090 packets/s and lost 50 s):
  0.1.6.0 kept about 7900 packets/s and lost 2 to 6 s in 300 s, about as
  much as with no load on some runs. The underruns there come from the
  rig's timing as much as from the load.
- Against its paired 0.1.6.0 control, HEAD cut Ring Underrun 5 to 8 times
  and left about 2.1 s lost in each pair; builds 20 and 22 (the real-time
  worker) cut it 8 to 28 times under load, to 17 to 67, leaving about 1 s
  lost on 98 SE; build 24 on ME cut it 6 to 10 times, leaving 0.5 s.
- No pair compared HEAD with a real-time build directly, and the same
  0.1.6.0 control varied between sessions (no load: 2367 in the HEAD
  session, 208 and 221 later). So the incremental effect of the real-time
  priority over the queue alone is suggested by these numbers, not
  measured; each row stands only against its own control.
- On 2000 a normal-priority busy thread does not disturb 0.1.6.0 in this
  rig (a handful of underruns in 300 s either way, under 0.1 s lost), so
  the comparison there shows no difference: build 24 is neither better nor
  worse. The 2000 pairs ran at 321 s of host time each, the 98 SE and ME
  ones at 326 to 360 s.
- Whether NTKERN honours the `IO_SOUND_INCREMENT` on the worker's wake
  (HEAD) cannot be told apart from the queue's own effect by this method;
  what was measured is the effect of each build as a whole.
- `KeSetPriorityThread` (build 20) and `KeQueryPerformanceCounter`
  (build 24) are resolved by 98 SE's and ME's NTKERN: both builds loaded
  and played (`uac2wdm.sys` of build 24, 47744 bytes, checked in place on
  both). After every run of builds 22 and 24 the stats read
  `RealtimeRead` 1, `Realtime` 1, `WorkerPriority` 16, `WorkerThrottles`
  0 (build 20: `Realtime` 1, `WorkerPriority` 16, no throttle word).

## The update from 0.1.6.0 (Update Driver, HEAD's INF)

- 98 SE: "Search for a better driver" with HEAD's folder as the location
  kept the installed driver ("The best driver Windows found is already
  installed", `INF\OTHER\USB-WD~1.INF`), although the folder's INF is
  0.1.8.0 against 0.1.6.0. "Display a list of all the drivers" > Have
  Disk > `D:\NEW` installed it, with these prompts: "Windows detects that
  your current driver may be a closer match ... Are you sure" (Yes);
  "Insert Disk ... UAC2 experimental build folder" (OK); Copying Files for
  `ks.cat` (skipped), `ks.sys` (from the 98 SE CD's files), `ksvpintf.ax`
  (skipped), `uac2wdm.sys` (from the build folder), `wdmaudio.cat`
  (skipped); "To finish setting up your new hardware, you must restart"
  (No, then Shut Down and a cold start). Afterwards `Uac2QueueMs` read 32
  (`REG_BINARY`, 4 bytes, as the INF writes it on 9x) and the stats
  `QueueMs` 32, `DataTransfers` 8. 98 SE keeps no `DriverVersion` value.
  Builds 20, 21, 22 and 24 went over the earlier build the same way.
- ME: the search route found `D:\NEW\UAC2WDM.INF` and installed it with
  no prompt and no restart prompt; `DriverVersion` 0.1.8.0, `QueueMs` 32,
  `DataTransfers` 8, a 5 s play PASS at once. The first 0.1.6.0 install
  asked for `ks.sys` (given `C:\WINDOWS\SYSTEM32\DRIVERS`) and
  `ksproxy.ax` (given the 98 SE CD's files), then "Version Conflict" for
  ME's newer KS files (kept). Build 24 over HEAD by Have Disk: the same
  file prompts, the closer-match warning, kept the newer KS files.
- 2000: the search route with HEAD's folder kept the installed driver ("A
  suitable driver for this device is already installed", `oem2.inf`).
  Display a list > Have Disk > `D:\NEW`: "Digital Signature Not Found"
  (Yes), "The hardware you installed will not work until you restart",
  Device Manager showed 0.1.8.0 at once; on closing the properties "Your
  hardware settings have changed. You must restart" (No, then Shut Down
  and a cold start). No STOP; `DriverVersion` 0.1.8.0, `Uac2QueueMs`
  `REG_DWORD` 32, `DataTransfers` 8, play PASS.
- 0.1.6.0 control twins: a Have Disk "downgrade" from HEAD to 0.1.6.0's
  INF left HEAD's `uac2wdm.sys` in place on ME (the newer file kept); the
  ME control twin's file was put back from the 0.1.6.0 folder by hand
  (control only, not the update step). On 2000 the same downgrade put the
  0.1.6.0 file (46048 bytes) in place.

## Regression (98 SE, HEAD `9f30c79`)

- CX31993: play 16-bit 48 kHz and 24-bit 96 kHz PASS, ramp with volume
  and mute PASS. `UACSEEK wo s16 48000 3 2000 p`: the simulator's capture
  holds 0 stale blocks; UACSEEK's own result was not kept (`S9.BAT` wrote it
  to `C:\S.LOG` in the guest and did not send it to COM1), so its API
  steps' success is not verified.
- `Uac2QueueMs` 128: `DataTransfers` 32; 16: 4; deleted: 32 ms, 8
  (`QueueRead` `FFFFFFFF`, the default). A play PASS at each.
- `hs24` (a new device instance, build 22's INF taken from `INF\OTHER`
  with no prompt): play PASS, record PASS with signal present, ramp PASS.
- ME and 2000: 5 s plays only (PASS on 0.1.6.0, HEAD and build 24), and
  build 24's three 2000 conditions and three ME conditions above.
- 2000, build 24: loaded after its update (`uac2wdm.sys` 47744 bytes in
  place, `DriverVersion` 0.1.8.0), a 5 s play PASS in 5017 ms,
  `WorkerPriority` 16 and `WorkerThrottles` 0 after every run.

## Rig traps

- 9x batches must have CRLF line ends, and a `rem` line with `<`, `>` or
  `|` is run as a redirection.
- XHCITR is a Windows program on 9x: `XHCITR /snapshot` opens a message
  box and writes nothing to a redirect; `start /w XHCITR /report <file>`
  then `find` works.
- One batch per condition; a finished DOS window is closed with Alt+Space,
  C (Alt+F4 on the desktop opens Shut Down). `choice` in a batch left a
  window open waiting.
- A simulator started with `--pcm-max` ends when the capture is full: the
  device disappears ("no UAC2 output").
