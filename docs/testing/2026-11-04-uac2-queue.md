# uac2 under CPU and disk load: the queue depth and the worker's wake, 2026-11-04

A user report (XP x64 SP2, AMD Phenom II X4 955, a Fresco FL1100 card on
a shared level interrupt, xHCI98 3.4.0.0, a Conexant CX31993 DAC on
`uac2` 0.1.6.0): the sound pops often under heavy CPU load (a game).
xHCI98's snapshot from that system: 7743 Ring Underrun (completion code
14) and 3 Missed Service (23) completions on the DAC's isochronous OUT
endpoint, a longest completion pass of 16041 us and a longest
publication of 10703 us on the bulk path (a UAS drive behind a hub on the
same controller), interrupt moderation 40 us. The change is design
record section 19 (`docs/design/uac2.md`). Development legs in QEMU
(TCG) with the simulator, not "Tested": no real CX31993, no game.

## Rig

- Guest: XP x64 SP2, a q35 overlay of the shared clean base, 2 vCPUs,
  2 GB. xHCI98 built from its 3.4.1.0 tree at `8b962d4e` (release,
  amd64; `xhci98.sys` `2686cc60...`; the isochronous and completion code
  is unchanged since 3.4.0.0), unsigned, installed with `INSTALL.BAT`.
- Emulator: stock QEMU 11.1.0 cannot run a High Speed `bInterval` 1
  endpoint in real time (a 5 s play took 15.0 s, 0 Ring Underrun: the
  model ran about two TDs a millisecond). The legs ran on the usb4-emu
  QEMU fork build `cb36497` (its isochronous real-time fix and
  `usb-redir`), where a 5 s play took 5000 ms.
- On `qemu-xhci`: the simulator `uacemu.py --profile cx31993` (High Speed,
  one synchronous playback endpoint, `bInterval` 1) through `usb-redir`
  on port 1, and a UAS disk (`usb-uas`, a 128 MiB FAT image) on port 4.
- Load, in the guest: CPU, one `cmd` busy loop per processor at normal
  priority; disk, two copy loops on the UAS disk (a 32 MiB file copied
  and deleted, and 200 files of 16 KiB copied and deleted).
- A play is `UACWAV play 2 16 48000 60` (WinMM, eight 7.5 s buffers, 16-bit
  stereo at 48 kHz; it gives up after 70 s). Counted per condition:
  xHCI98's completion codes from `XHCITR64 /snapshot` before and after,
  the simulator's OUT packets per second of play (8000 is real time) and
  its gaps between successive OUT packets (`gap_ms`, this change), and
  the time each play took beyond 60 s.
- Drivers, `uac2wdm.sys` x64 swapped between conditions with the device
  unplugged: 0.1.6.0 as released (`c21c9425...`); the queue alone,
  `161320d` (`b7d221fa...`); the queue and the wake increment, `502a8a7`
  (`91969ddf...`); and a test-only build of `502a8a7` whose worker raises
  itself to `LOW_REALTIME_PRIORITY` (`dea583d9...`, never released).
  `Uac2QueueMs` absent (the default, 32 ms: 8 data URBs of 4 ms).

## The load legs

port 1; 10 plays with CPU and disk load, 5 with each alone and 5 with
none; the driver swapped between sets. "Incomplete": the play's eight
buffers were not all played back after 70 s. "Time lost": the plays'
time beyond 60 s each, summed (for the incomplete plays, to the 70 s
give-up).

- **CPU load drives it; disk load alone does not.** The old driver under
  CPU load got about half the stream out in time (4093 packets a second),
  one Ring Underrun per gap of 10 ms or more: each was the refill
  thread waiting a scheduling quantum behind the busy loops. Disk load
  alone (the UAS copies on the same controller) gave the idle numbers.
- **The queue alone** (8 URBs, 32 ms) kept every play complete and cut
  the long gaps by two orders of magnitude, but left thousands of short
  Ring Underruns (4709 under CPU load, only 97 gaps of 5 ms or more).
  Under xHCI98 a pipe has four transfer records (19.7): the controller's
  ring holds at most four URBs, 16 ms; the four behind them wait in
  xHCI98 and are published as records retire.
- **The wake increment** (`IO_SOUND_INCREMENT`) brought CPU load down to
  62 Ring Underruns in five minutes (34 in ten with disk load as well),
  close to idle; the test-only real-time worker gave 13 and 32. The
  boost is the change kept; the real-time priority is not part of it
  (19.6).
- **Missed Service**: none in any condition (the user's snapshot had 3).
- **xHCI98's longest passes** in the snapshots, over the whole session:
  publication 10.2 ms, completion pass 10.1 ms (both under TCG, where a
  vCPU can be descheduled by the host inside a pass); no pass of 16 ms.
  The user's figures are converted at the time-stamp counter's mean rate
  over the controller's 37805 s (763.3 counts a microsecond, on a
  3.2 GHz processor): a counter that slows with the processor's power
  states makes a pass timed at full clock read up to about four times
  too long.

## `Uac2QueueMs` and the regression legs

- XP x86 (q35 overlay, xHCI98 3.4.0.0 as released, x86, unsigned;
  `uac2` x86 of `502a8a7` from `w2k-xp`'s INF, `9a3283bc...` of
  `9f30c79` for the last two rows): installed from `oem3.inf` at the first
  plug, `DriverVersion` 0.1.8.0, the INF wrote `Uac2QueueMs` `REG_DWORD`
  0x20. CX31993: play 16-bit 48 kHz and 24-bit 96 kHz 5 s each in about
  5.1 s, ramp with volume and mute PASS, `UACSEEK wo s16 48000 3 2000 p`
  PASS with 0 stale blocks. `Uac2QueueMs` 128: 29 data URBs with
  `502a8a7`'s ring (the reason for `9f30c79`), 32 with `9f30c79`; 16: 4;
  32: 8 (stats `QueueRead`, `QueueMs`, `DataTransfers`, `TransferUs`
  4000). `hs24`: play, record (signal present) and ramp PASS.
- XP x64 (`a1` after the load legs, cold start, `uac2wdm.sys` x64 of
  `9f30c79`, `f2dbf507...`, in place of the installed 0.1.6.0 file): CX31993
  under xHCI98: play 16-bit 48 kHz and 24-bit 96 kHz PASS (5000 and
  5016 ms), ramp PASS, `UACSEEK wo s16 48000 3 2000 p` PASS with 0 stale
  blocks; `Uac2QueueMs` 128: 32 data URBs, 16: 4. The CX31993 on Windows'
  EHCI (`ich9-usb-ehci1`, a new device instance): play and ramp PASS.
  Installing a new device instance from 0.1.6.0's INF over the swapped
  file asked "Confirm File Replace" (the target newer than the source),
  answered No: an artefact of the swap, not of the change. `hs24` on
  xHCI98 (installed the same way): record (signal present), play and
  ramp PASS.
