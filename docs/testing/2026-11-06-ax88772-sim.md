# ax88772 with a simulated AX88772B/C: 98 SE and ME, 2026-11-06

xHCI98 roadmap 47.26 (owner, 2026-11-06): the ASIX AX88772B and AX88772C
on 98 SE and ME, which ASIX's drivers leave out (ethernet.md section 6.8).
No such adapter is on the bench, so the legs ran against
`src/net/ax88772/ax7emu.py`, a simulator written from the same Linux files
as the driver (`asix_common.c`, `asix_devices.c`, `asix.h` at v7.3). A
misreading that the driver and the simulator share passes here: the real
chips stay owed (below). All of this is emulated: no physical device was
used and none is claimed.

## The driver and its host tests

- Source at `8b3504e` (branch `w47-wdm4`). The design was reviewed and
  revised before the code; the code was reviewed and revised until no
  material finding was left.
- `test.cmd`: `chiptest` 58837 checks (the bring-up order, MII host
  enable, link resolution and pause, the filter, the transmit pad word,
  the receive stream cut at every even offset into two and three
  transfers, split headers, lost synchronisation, refused records, the
  realignment after lost data, the retries of a Link, link-down step,
  address or filter that failed part way), `coretest` 34568 checks (the
  shared core with this package's 2048-byte chip state). `ax7simtest.py`:
  12 tests of the chip core through the simulator's usbredir paths (ARP,
  ICMP up to 4000 bytes, records crossing transfers, random cuts, a
  transfer lost in continuous traffic, the filter, Stop and Resume).
- `build.cmd ndis5 trace` (the free build plus a trace to QEMU's port
  0xE9, test-only): `ax772f.sys` SHA-256 `b96127f9...1c67222`. The free
  build: `06d06232...c933e`. `AX772F98.INF` `9d99d320...0b4c6`.

## Rig

- Stock QEMU 11.1.0, pc machine, TCG, one vCPU per guest, headless;
  `qemu-xhci` and `usb-ehci` on each guest.
- 98 SE: an overlay of `w98-nusb-hub.qcow2` (98 SE with NUSB 3.3 and
  xHCI98 3.1.0.0, the `rtl815x` simulator legs' base). The wizard for
  this PCI layout's EHCI controller was taken to the end: it installed
  NUSB's `USB2.INF`. xHCI98 updated to 3.4.1.0 as released (the kit in
  xHCI98's `releases/3.4.1.0`) with `INSTALL /console /yes`: exit 3, then
  a cold start; `XHCI98.SYS` 337,499 bytes.
- ME: an overlay of `me-hcd310.qcow2` (ME with xHCI98 3.1.0.0), updated to
  3.4.1.0 the same way. The installer exits 4 there because the base's
  registry holds an old entry for an xHCI controller at another PCI
  address with no driver; its own report says the controller in use
  already runs 3.4.1.0. ME has no EHCI driver; its wizard for the EHCI
  controller was finished with none.
- The driver from a virtual floppy, through the hardware wizard. Each
  plug started a new simulator process with its own log. The simulator
  ran at High Speed; `--flap-after`/`--flap-for` took the cable out once.

## Not run

- 2000 and later: ASIX's own driver serves these chips there (no NT INF).
- Full Speed, 10 Mb/s and half-duplex links in a guest (host tests only),
  multicast traffic, standby and resume, throughput.
- The free build in a guest (the trace build is the same code with a
  trace).

## Owed to the bench

A real AX88772B and AX88772C on 98 SE (xHCI98 and NUSB's EHCI) and ME:
install from `AX772F98.INF`, the trace lines "chip start: 0, version 1020"
and "link 1", a DHCP lease (`winipcfg`), `ping -n 20 -l 1472` and
`-l 4000` to the gateway, a cable pull and replug during `ping -t`, and
removal. What the simulator cannot show: whether the chip's bulk-IN
transfers end with the 2 KiB burst (the driver reads 2048-byte transfers,
as Linux), whether turning receive off and on empties its buffer (the
realignment after lost data), the PHY's own timing and LPA behaviour, and
the chip's interrupt report (inferred from the AX88172's).
