# uac2 0.1.8.26 on XP x64: swapping USB audio devices under VLC, 2026-11-06

A user reported, on XP x64 with `uac2`, that "swapping to a different USB
audio device needs VLC restarted", and later that a swap to or from a UAC
1.0 device (`usbaudio`) needs no restart (xHCI98 roadmap task 47.11).
The 2026-11-05 legs of build 24 ([uac2-xp64-exhaustive](2026-11-05-uac2-xp64-exhaustive.md))
swapped devices while VLC played and found no difference between `uac2`
and `usbaudio`: VLC reached the new device at its next file or loop, with
either driver. This record repeats the swap on the released 0.1.8.26
with the steps a user takes between files (VLC stopped, the device
swapped, Play again) and with the swap made while VLC plays, then with two
devices attached and Windows' default device in play; it finds the
difference and checks the change made for it ([uac2.md](../design/uac2.md)
section 22). Development legs in QEMU (TCG), not a release and not
"Tested".

  vCPU, on the usb4-emu fork emulator `cb36497` (`usb-host`, `usb-redir`,
  the isochronous real-time fix). xHCI98 3.4.1.0 (the released `nt-x64`,

  `uac2wdm.inf` `ccc7efb7...`), staged with `SetupCopyOEMInf`. The
  overlay used for the legs is a second overlay of that one.
- **Devices:** the host's Creative Sound Blaster X4 (`041E:3278`) and X1
  (`041E:3286`), both UAC 2.0 under `uac2`, and the Logi USB Headset
  (`046D:0A64`), UAC 1.0 under Windows' `usbaudio`, the control. Each was
  attached by QMP `device_add usb-host,hostbus=..,hostaddr=..` (the bus and
  address read from `info usbhost` at each attach), one guest at a time,
  with a usbmon capture per attach. On first plug the X4 installed as
  "Sound Blaster X4" and "Sound Blaster X4 2" (two playback pairs) and the
  X1 as "Sound Blaster X1"; the Logi as "Logi USB Headset".
  - The guest's driver policy was set to not search Windows Update; the
    "Windows Logo testing" prompts for the xHCI98 composite parent and
    `uac2` were answered Continue Anyway. A prompt answered Stop by a key

- **Simulated:** `uacemu.py --profile cx31993m`, the reporter's CX31993
  with a MAX97220 (`3302:12C3` layout, adaptive playback), on `usb-redir`,
  its received PCM written to a file.
- **Player:** VLC 3.0.11 win32 (`vlc.exe` `fac5ae20...`; 3.0.21 does not
  start on XP x64, as the 2026-11-05 record found), `--loop` over a
  3-minute 48 kHz 16-bit stereo tone, `--aout=directsound` (VLC's default
  output on XP: its `mmdevice` and `wasapi` modules do not load there) or
  `--aout=waveout`, Windows' default device in each case.

## Steps of one case

1. Device A attached; 40 s later VLC started on the tone.
2. After 35 s, A's rate (bytes per second of its capture over 10 s).
3. `stopplay`: VLC's Stop (`s`) first. `mid`: VLC left playing.
4. A removed (`device_del`), 6 s later device B attached; 45 s later B's
   rate ("after swap").
5. VLC's Stop, then Play (space): a new audio output in the same VLC
   process. B's rate over 10 s from 12 s after Play, and again from 37 s
   after ("after stop and play").
6. VLC ended and started again; B's rate 35 s later ("after restart").

1.6 to 1.7 MB/s is the X4 or X1 playing (2 channels, 16-bit, 48 kHz in
High Speed isochronous packets, with the capture's own overhead); about
360 KB/s the Logi playing; 0 nothing sent to the device.

- In every case the new device played after VLC's Stop and Play, without
  restarting VLC: `uac2` to `uac2` (X4 and X1 both ways, the CX31993 and
  the X1 both ways), `uac2` to `usbaudio` and back, DirectSound and
  waveOut, VLC stopped during the swap or playing through it.
- Right after the swap the new device got nothing in every case, with
  either driver: VLC keeps its open output on the device that is gone (its
  timeline runs on). VLC's log shows `closing audio device` and `Opening
  DirectSound Audio Output` at Stop and Play only, never at the device
  change (VLC 3.0.11 on XP has no output module that follows a device
  change, as the 2026-11-05 record found).
- Each new device was the only one attached, so Windows made it the
  default playback device and VLC's Default output reached it.

## Two devices attached: Windows' default device

`dflt.sh`: device A attached, 40 s later device B; VLC started
(DirectSound, Default); Windows' default playback device changed by hand
(Sounds and Audio Devices, Audio, Default device); VLC's Stop and Play;
then VLC restarted. Rates as above, A / B.

| case | A, then B | VLC on start | default changed to | after the change | after Stop, Play | after VLC restart |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| d1 | X1, then Logi | 0 / 366 KB/s (the Logi) | Logi (no change) | 0 / 351 KB/s | 0 / 363 KB/s | 0 / 362 KB/s |
| d2 | Logi, then X1 | 361 KB/s / 0 (the Logi) | X1 | 363 KB/s / 0 | 365 KB/s / 0 | 0 / 1.67 MB/s |
| d3 | Logi, then X1; X1 made default before VLC | 0 / 1.44 MB/s (the X1) | Logi | 0 / 1.44 MB/s | 0 / 1.44 MB/s | 363 KB/s / 0 |

- **The one difference between the drivers.** The Logi (`usbaudio`)
  attached after the X1 became Windows' default at once (d1); the X1
  (`uac2`) attached after the Logi did not (d2): the Sound panel still
  named the Logi, and VLC's Default output played to it.
- **A default changed by hand is not followed by a running VLC**, at Stop
  and Play, in either direction: to the X1 (d2) and to the Logi (d3); a
  VLC restart reached it in both. That is VLC on XP, the same for both
  drivers.
- Together: on a PC with another sound device, a UAC 1.0 device takes the
  sound when it is installed and a `uac2` device does not; the user then
  picks the `uac2` device by hand, and VLC needs a restart to follow. That
  this is what the user met is a likely reading, not a reproduction of the
  user's PC.

## Where the difference comes from

Windows' own USB audio INF (`wdma_usb.inf`, read as text in the guest)
writes `HKR,,SetupPreferredAudioDevices,3,01,00,00,00` in a device's
driver key; Microsoft's driver kit documentation says it makes the device
the default for playback, recording and MIDI when its driver is
installed. `uac2wdm.inf` 0.1.8.26 has no such line. In the guest, the X1's
driver key held `SetupPreferredAudioDevicesCount` 0 (written by Windows).
Adding the value by hand to the already installed X1's key changed
nothing at its next arrival: Windows acts on it at install. Design:
[uac2.md](../design/uac2.md) section 22.

## The change, read on XP x64

`uac2wdm.inf` with `HKR,,SetupPreferredAudioDevices,3,02,00,00,00` in
`[AudioSupport]` (`2dc6523`) and `DriverVer` 11/06/2026,0.1.9.0 so that
Windows prefers it to the staged 0.1.8.26: the branch's
`src/audio/uac2/x64/uac2wdm.inf` byte for byte (`30b34655...`), with the
release's `uac2wdm.sys` (`9b74218f...`, unchanged), staged with
`SetupCopyOEMInf` beside 0.1.8.26. In each case the Logi was
attached and Windows' default first; then a device new to the system was
installed; the Audio tab of Sounds and Audio Devices was read.

| case | device installed | INF | default playback after | default recording after | MIDI | VLC (Default), started on the Logi before the install |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p1 | simulated CX31993 with a MAX97220 (new id) | changed | that device | the Logi (the device has no capture) | GS synth, unchanged | at Stop and Play: the device 197 KB/s, the Logi 0 |
| p2 | simulated `hs24`, duplex (new id) | changed | that device | that device | unchanged | - |
| p3 | simulated `x4full`, two playback pairs (new id) | changed | "UAC2 X4 layout - SIMULATED", pair 0 | the same | unchanged | VLC started after the install played on the device's interface 4 (pair 0, the analog output) |
| r1 | the real X1, new to that overlay | changed | Sound Blaster X1 | Sound Blaster X1 | unchanged | the X1 1.68 MB/s, the Logi 0 (VLC's loop point fell after the install; its log shows the output reopened there), and again after Stop and Play |
| c1 (control) | simulated CX31993 with a MAX97220 (new id), on an overlay with 0.1.8.26 only | released | the Logi | the Logi | unchanged | - |
| u1 (update) | the real X1, installed earlier with 0.1.8.26, updated to the changed INF (`UpdateDriverForPlugAndPlayDevices`) | changed | the Logi, unchanged | the Logi, unchanged | unchanged | - |

- With the change, a `uac2` device new to the system becomes Windows'
  default when it is installed, as a UAC 1.0 device under `usbaudio`
  does; without it (c1) it does not.
- An update over a device already installed leaves the user's defaults
  alone (u1). So a user who already has `uac2` keeps today's behaviour for
  devices already installed; a device installed after the update takes
  the default.
- On a device with several pairs the default is pair 0 (p3).
- The MIDI default stayed Windows' GS synth in every case.
- Screens: `shots\pref-p1-mmsys.png`, `pref-p2-mmsys.png`,
  `pref-p3-mmsys.png`, `real-x1-mmsys.png`, `ctl-c1-mmsys.png`,
  `upd-2-mmsys.png`.

## The change on 98 SE (2026-11-06, simulators only)

The real devices were not used (another task had them). Rig: 98 SE (an
overlay of the usbcom kit's guest, NUSB 3.3's EHCI and 98 SE's
`USBHUB.SYS`, updated to xHCI98 3.4.1.0 with `INSTALL.BAT /console`),
`pc` machine, 1 vCPU, the usb4-emu fork emulator `cb36497`, `qemu-xhci`
and `usb-ehci`. Simulated devices: `uacemu.py --profile hs24` (duplex)
under new ids and product names "UAC2 A" to "UAC2 D" (`--product`), on
`usb-redir`. `uac2` from `D:\UOLD` (the release's `w98se-me` folder,
0.1.8.26) or `D:\UNEW` (the branch's `uac2wdm.inf`, `aab14cf1...`, with
the release's `uac2wdm.sys`). 98 SE's KS and WDM audio files came from
the 98 SE CD's cabinets (`EXTRACT`) into `C:\KSX`; the catalog files it
asked for were skipped. Read: Control Panel, Multimedia, Audio, the
preferred playback and recording devices.

98 SE notes, read on the way:

- 98 SE keeps one copy of the INF in `INF\OTHER` by provider and name
  (`usb-wdm-freeuac2wdm.inf`). A search of `D:\UNEW` after `D:\UOLD` had
  been installed still installed the copy in `INF\OTHER`, the released
  one; the changed INF went in only by Have Disk ("Display a list",
  Have Disk `D:\UNEW`), which replaced that copy.
- After acting on the value Windows sets it to 0 in the device's driver
  key (`SetupPreferredAudioDevices=hex:00,00,00,00`, regedit export);
  XP x64 left no value behind.
- An update by Device Manager asked for a restart.
- The preferred devices are kept by name; after a cold start, with the
  simulated devices attached one after another within seconds of QEMU's
  start, they were not always the ones chosen before (below).

### What this adds

- The change does on 98 SE, under both host controller drivers, what it
  does on XP x64: a `uac2` device installed with it becomes the preferred
  device; one installed with the released INF does not.
- 98 SE differs in two ways. It installs further devices from its one
  `INF\OTHER` copy, so the changed INF reaches a system that already has
  `uac2` only by Have Disk (or Update Driver) once; and updating the
  preferred device itself moved the preference to another device while
  the driver was replaced, with the released INF as with the changed one
  (the control above). The move lasted past the next start once (step 6,
  D on the changed INF); with D on the released INF (e4) and in a repeat
  of steps 1 to 6 from a clean state with D on the changed INF (r7) the
  choice made by hand came back after the start. So after updating the
  preferred device's driver on 98 SE, the user may have to check the
  Multimedia panel; that the change causes the move to last is not
  shown.
- Not run: ME and 2000 (no prepared image with `uac2`), the real devices.
- Evidence: `E:\xhci98-vm\a347\wdm\ser\` - `shots\ux-*.png` and
  `un-*.png` (the Multimedia panels `ux-mm-*`, `un-mm-*`), `ue-*.png`,
  `ur-*.png`, the regedit readings `ux-reg*`, `un-reg1`, `ur-12-reg*`, `pids.txt`, `evidence\u-*.jsonl`,
  `l98.ps1` and `scripts\` (`s98.sh`, `mm98.sh`, `up98.sh`).
