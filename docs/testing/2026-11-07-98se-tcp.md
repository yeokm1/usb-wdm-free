# Sustained TCP on 98 SE: the stalls and faults of the 98 TCP/IP, 2026-11-07

Owner, 2026-11-07: chase the fault of the 98 SE TCP/IP (`MSTCP`) under
sustained TCP over this repository's network drivers before the next
release. It was found with a real RTL8157 (`2026-11-07-rtl8157-hardware.md`,
"98 SE, still open"): every build stalled within seconds to minutes of
sustained TCP, and the 98 TCP/IP took fatal exceptions 0E, with the
driver's own queues and receive pool intact. That device was in use
elsewhere, so this run used the simulators only; no adapter is claimed.

## Rig

- Stock QEMU 11.1.0, `pc` machine, TCG, one vCPU, 256 MB, headless; each
  leg on a fresh overlay of the 98 SE or ME image of
  `2026-11-06-netcore-port.md` (NUSB 3.3, xHCI98 3.4.1.0, the four
  drivers installed), the new image copied over `C:\WINDOWS\SYSTEM` with
  the device unplugged; the simulator over usbredir on `qemu-xhci` port 1.
- New in the simulators (`rtlemu.py`, `axemu.py`, `ax7emu.py`,
  `sremu.py`): `--udp LOCAL:REMOTE` sends every frame the guest transmits
  as one UDP datagram and delivers every datagram received as a frame,
  the framing of QEMU's `socket,udp=` backend. QEMU joins that backend
  and a user-mode network (`-netdev user`, with a host port forwarded to
  the guest) on one hub, so the guest talks real TCP through the
  simulated chip. Frames are then not logged one by one.
- Traffic: `NETPERF.EXE` in the guest, `netperf.py` on the host through
  the forwarded port (`2026-11-07-rtl8157-hardware.md`, "Rig"): 30 s
  host to guest ("in"), 20 s guest to host ("out"), then both at once.
  The stream is a checked pattern; a stall shows as the host's 30 s
  receive or send timeout.
- Control: the same 98 SE (the CD's own install, no USB drivers) with
  QEMU's `pcnet` and 98 SE's in-box driver (`PCNTN4M.SYS`, AMD PCNET
  Family) on the same kind of user-mode network.
- XP: a fresh overlay of the XP image of `2026-11-06-netcore-port.md`
  (xHCI98 3.4.1.0, `rtl815x` installed), the same bridge, `rtlemu.py
  --chip 8157` at SuperSpeed.

The 98 SE TCP/IP itself carries about ten times the load through an
NDIS driver of its own system, so the fault is in how these drivers
drive it. ME did not show it in 420 s of both ways.

## The experiments

- **ReceiveComplete.** `NdisMEthIndicateReceiveComplete` after each
  bulk-IN transfer's indications: the receive still stalled (second row
  above). Not it.
- **Upcalls from an NDIS timer.** Every receive indication and send
  completion queued by the USB completion and made from an NDIS
  miniport timer routine (`NdisMSetTimer` with no delay): in 20 s at
  12.1 Mb/s, out 12.4 Mb/s, 180 s both ways (201 MB in, 110 MB out),
  0 mismatched, no stall.

## The change

`netcore.c` (identical in the four packages), x86 NDIS 5.0 build only,
on 98 SE and ME only (ethernet.md section 4.4, "Upcalls on 98 SE and ME").
NT keeps the direct calls; the NDIS 5.1 (x64) build has none of the code:
the x64 images built before and after it are equal in every section
(`.text`, `.rdata`, `.data`, `.pdata`, `INIT`, `.rsrc`, `.reloc`), only
the PE time stamp differs.

Host tests: `test.cmd` PASS in each package (35612 core checks; new:
`Deferred98`, `DeferredHalt98` with 17 and 64 queued receives; the fake
NDIS counts any indication or send completion from inside a USB
completion on 98 SE/ME as a violation). The four simulators' own tests
(`rtlsimtest.py`, `axsimtest.py`, `ax7simtest.py`, `sr9simtest.py`) PASS.

## Sleep requests (review, after the first legs)

A sleep request (`OID_PNP_SET_POWER` to D1-D3) completed while the timer
could still indicate a queued receive. Now the delivery stops and drains
before a sleep request completes, and resumes before a D0 request
reaches the worker, whose restart sends; while asleep a send that fails
at once completes at once (ethernet.md section 4.4). Host tests:
`Power98` in each package's `coretest.c`. The four packages' x64 images
are again equal in every section.

### The send queue and removal (owner, 2026-11-07)

Ported next from the shared core: the send queue without a limit
(`f446180`) and the departure reported before any transfer wait
(`16333c0`). On XP the removal fix alone did not act: xHCI98 holds a
departed device's transfers, so none completes, and ECM and NCM make no
control request of their own (the core finds the departure with its 2 s
link poll). `cdcusb.c` now asks the device for its status every 2 s of
a quiet worker; a device-gone answer reports the departure.

### The two-way stall on 98 SE/ME, second time box (owner, 2026-11-07)

Hypothesis tried: on 98 SE/ME an NDIS timer set again from inside its
own routine is lost, which would leave `UpcallArmed` TRUE (no delivery
in either direction) and make Halt wait for ever. Branch `w47-tcp98b`,
`f417ef2`, removes that case in the shared core and in `cdcnet`: the
routine delivers until both queues are empty and only then clears
`UpcallArmed`, and two timers are set in turn (reviewed: the first
form left a re-arm window after the final unlock, which the second timer
closes). The x64 images are equal in every section before and after.

300 s both ways, a fresh boot each:

| System | Driver, device | Build | Runs | Stalled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 98 SE | `rtl815x`, 8153 simulator, xHCI98 | `f417ef2` | 4 | 0 (173 to 231 MB out, 375 to 509 MB in) |
| ME | `ax772f`, AX88772B simulator, xHCI98 | `f417ef2` | 4 | 0 (136 to 187 MB out, 235 to 324 MB in) |
| ME | `cdcnet` RNDIS, QEMU `usb-net`, UHCI | `f5568e9` (earlier) | 5 | 1, after about 100 s |
| ME | the same | `f417ef2` | 5 | 4, after 80 to 215 s |
| ME | the same | `9d0ef33` (`w47-rel019`) | 2 | 1, after about 127 s |

So the change did not remove the stall: with `usb-net` on ME it came
as often with the change as without it, or more often. The runs share
the host with other work, and its load moves the guest's processor load,
so the rates are not compared beyond that. Removing the re-arm case was
not enough to prevent the stall. Whether that case played a part in the
earlier stalls is not shown either way: no stalled-state evidence ties
the stalls of the old and the new builds to one cause. `f417ef2` closes
a window the timer rules leave open, and it is in the 0.1.9 release
branch (owner, 2026-11-07) as that, not as a fix of the stall.

What the stall looks like on ME (`cdcnet`, `usb-net`, UHCI):

- The last five runs (three of `f417ef2`, the two of `9d0ef33`) were
  each followed by a new connection each way (recv 30 s, then send
  20 s). After the three stalled ones (two and one) these passed
  nothing: about 1.2 MB taken by the host side's own buffers, and 0
  bytes the other way. After the two clean ones they ran at the usual
  rates. On XP under xHCI98 the held data went out as soon as the
  inbound stream ended, and a new connection ran at once; on ME the
  network stays dead.
- The guest itself was not frozen: the taskbar clock moved on through
  the stall.
- A test-only trace build (a state line from the worker thread at
  every fourth 1 s timeout, never released) shows, while data flows,
  the upcall timer working (its run count rising with the frames,
  `UpcallArmed` set and cleared), receive packets returned (`RxOut` 0
  to 4 of 64), the four receive transfers and the one send transfer in
  flight, and up to 44 sends queued behind the single bulk-OUT transfer
  (one frame per transfer: QEMU's `usb-net` takes one RNDIS packet per
  message). In its one stalled run (`9d0ef33`) the lines stop, and
  none follow through the probes and the removal. Those lines come only
  from a timeout, so this shows only that the timed-out waits stopped:
  the worker may instead be blocked elsewhere in its loop (its recovery,
  requests, RNDIS control exchanges, probe or callbacks, some of which
  wait themselves, and `Sync` waits without a limit after a cancel), or
  be woken without pause so that no wait times out. Where it is was not
  traced (that needs entry and exit lines or a thread stack).
- In the same five runs the device was then removed 15 s before the
  guest was stopped: after the two clean runs the trace shows "halt:
  begin", the upcall run count and "halted"; after the three stalled
  runs nothing, so NDIS did not call Halt.
- A lead, not checked: a stop of the kernel's timer delivery would
  explain the three together (the upcall timer's routine, which
  delivers both directions, the worker's timeouts, and the removal);
  so would a worker blocked in one of the waits above.
  The shared core's drivers use the same timer at a lower rate (several
  frames per transfer, at High or SuperSpeed); `cdcnet` on `usb-net`
  runs it about 400 times a second.

The shared core's drivers did not stall in the eight runs above.

Halt at shutdown and removal (asked because one 98 SE restart after
installing `ax179f` waited at "Windows is shutting down" for over 10
minutes): every removal at the end of the eight core runs above traced
"halt: begin", the upcall run count and "halted" (98 SE and ME,
`f417ef2`), and a 98 SE restart after traffic with the `w47-rel019`
`ax179f` traced "halt: 12046 upcall runs" and "halted". The restart
waited only while the NETPERF console was open; ending that task let
Windows go on. The long wait was not reproduced with the shared core.
`StopUpcalls` cancels the timer (both timers in `f417ef2`) and waits
only while the routine is set or running. A removal after the `cdcnet`
stall on ME did not reach Halt within the time observed (above). Why is
not known: sends still outstanding keep NDIS from unbinding and halting
(`cdcusb.c`, `ReleaseGone`), and the driver's callbacks and worker run
before Halt. A restart in that state was not tried, so where a shutdown
would wait, and whether that was the 98 SE restart's case, is not known.
