# The 0.1.9 matrix on the NT systems, 2026-11-07/08

xHCI98 Phase 47 (owner, 2026-11-07): before usb-wdm-free 0.1.9, every driver
on every NT system its `STAGE.TXT` lists, against the current simulators,
under xHCI98 and under Windows' own USB stack. This record holds the NT
half (2000, XP, Vista, 7, 8, 8.1; x86 and x64; no driver lists 10); 98 SE
and ME are a separate record. Everything here is emulated: simulators
over QEMU's `usb-redir` and QEMU's own device models. No physical device
was used and none is claimed.

The matrix ran on three builds, in this order:

1. **`e6526a6`** (`w47-rel019`, 2026-11-07): the whole matrix.
2. **`bf92407`** (the rebuild, 2026-11-08): the drivers it changed, rerun
   (`cdcnet`, `rtl815x`, `ax88772`, `sr9700`, `ax88179`, `absptr`, `uac2`
   under Windows' own stack) and a check that `uac1` does not bind on NT.
3. **`3df15d0`** (the release check passed; the owner-signed x64 images):
   a short confirmation on Vista and 7 x64 and the 8 x64 `uac2` question,
   with released 0.1.8.26 as the control.

`usbmidi` went in there from a TEST copy with a `CatalogFile` line and a
catalog made with MakeCat and signed in the guest, as 0.1.5.0's legs did.

## Rig

- QEMU 11.1.0 (stock, `usb-redir`), q35, TCG, headless, development host
  A; each leg set a fresh overlay of a base made here from the read-only
  q35 bases: one with xHCI98 installed (the board's `qemu-xhci`) and one
  with Windows' own stack (no `qemu-xhci` on 2000 to 7: `usb-ehci` and
  `piix3-usb-uhci`; on 8 and 8.1 Windows' own `usbxhci` on `qemu-xhci`
  beside `usb-ehci` and UHCI). High Speed devices on EHCI, Full Speed on
  UHCI, everything on xHCI98's port 1. One vCPU per leg guest, six guests
  at a time; the host ran near 99 % throughout.
- **`uac2` on the usb4-emu QEMU fork** `cb36497` (its isochronous
  real-time fix and `usb-redir`, the build 0.1.8's `uac2` records used),
  from 2026-11-07 19:30 for every `uac2` leg (coordinator). Stock QEMU's
  isochronous path stopped XP's EHCI playback and slowed xHCI98's
  playback to a third of real time (the side checks below); the earlier
  stock-QEMU `uac2` rows are kept as "stock" where no fork leg replaced
  them.
- A leg set is one driver on one system under one stack: the release
  folders copied in, the driver installed as its line says, then every
  profile of its simulator: plug, the device read (`devcon` on XP and
  later x86, `wmic` on x64, the `Enum` registry on 2000), the functional
  test, unplug; the first profile also unplugged, replugged and tested
  again; then the first profile plugged across a restart and tested again.
  On 2000 a restart is a shutdown and a cold start (a warm restart hangs on
  this q35 board), also when Setup asks for one (`uac2`'s 2000 INF always
  does; `usbmidi`'s TEST INF did, "files in use"). Install dialogs were
  answered by the harness from screenshots, as a person would.
- Functional tests: `uac2`: `UACWAV` play (2 ch 16-bit 48 kHz; 8 ch for
  `multi`) and record (the profile's capture format), PASS when every
  buffer completes and the recording holds the simulator's tone;
  `usbmidi`: `MIDITEST` loopback (32 notes and a 259-byte SysEx) on every
  input port against its output port; `uvc98`: `M1CAP`, three complete
  changing frames at 160x120, then 320x240 and 640x480; `xpad98`: `JOYTEST`
  (every button, both ends of every axis, the d-pad); `usbflop`: list and
  read every file and compare, quick-format, copy a 200,000-byte pattern
  file on and back and compare; `absptr`: six points and the left button
  (a pen's tip); `usbcom`: 1000 bytes looped at 9600 and 115200 on every
  COM port; network drivers: DHCP, `ping` at 32, 1472 and 4000 bytes, and
  TCP where the simulator serves it (`ax7emu`, `sremu`: a 256 KiB checked
  pattern down and 256 KiB up with its SHA-256 answered; QEMU's `usb-net`:
  `NETPERF` both ways through slirp; `axemu`, `rtlemu` and `ncmemu` serve no
  TCP, so `ax88179`, `rtl815x` and the NCM profiles are ping only).

## Findings

### Driver faults

1. **`uac2` `bf92407`, XP under Windows' EHCI: STOP 0xC2 BAD_POOL_CALLER
   (0x40, 0x00060000, 0x80000000, 0).** At the `x4` profile's plug, right
   after `x4full` passed: XP's arrival sound streamed IF4 alt 1 for about
   1 s (7,040 packets), the stream stopped and alternate 0 was set, and
   XP stopped. The `x4` model accepts only IF4 alt 1 and refuses the
   other alternates it advertises, so `uac2`'s path for a refused
   alternate is the first suspect. Not seen on `e6526a6` (stock QEMU), where
   `x4` passed. The rest of XP's `bf92407` profiles did not run. Evidence:
   `uac2@b-xp-win-11080529` (`dead-s011.png`, the model log). Reported at
   once; the final fix C (`3df15d0`) was not run on XP here.
2. **`uac2` under Windows' own stack, playback with an explicit feedback
   endpoint** (believed real, under investigation): 7 x86 on the fork
   plays `x4full` 1, 1 and 4 of 8 buffers, `multi` and `fs` 0 of 8, while
   every recording passes; Vista and 8 x86 show the same at times. The
   synchronous and adaptive profiles (`cx31993`, `cx31993m`, `adaptive`)
   pass. The XP pcap showed the guest stopping its iso OUT, not
   `usb-redir`. On the fork XP plays `x4full` and `multi` in real time, so
   XP's stock-QEMU failures were the rig.
3. **`uac2` Full Speed under Windows' UHCI** (believed real, under
   investigation): `fs`/`fs4` play 0 of 8 on XP, Vista, 7 and 8 under
   Windows' stack, on stock QEMU and the fork; recording passes. On 2000's
   UHCI (`e6526a6`) the device was never configured at all (only the hub's
   enumeration on the bus); on `bf92407` and the fork 2000's `fs` passed.
4. **`uac2` on 8 x64 under Windows' stack: every first-plug recording
   fails to open** (`open=1`), on `bf92407`, on the final fix C and on
   released 0.1.8.26 alike; after a restart the same recording passes.
   Not new in 0.1.9.
5. **`usbflop` on 7 x64**: reads, writes and compares pass; the quick
   format fails under both stacks (Windows' stack: "Error in IOCTL call";
   xHCI98: "Cannot open volume for direct access", and the first copy
   from the medium hit an I/O error). Format on 7 was in no earlier record.
   `usbflop` is unchanged since 0.1.8.
6. **`cdcnet` with QEMU's `usb-net` under xHCI98: guest-to-host TCP is
   slow**: XP 0.12 Mb/s, 2000 and XP x64 about 1.4 Mb/s against 110 to 150
   Mb/s host to guest; 7 x64 38 Mb/s; Windows' stack about 3 Mb/s both
   ways. The data is intact (no mismatch); the legs pass.

### Windows' own drivers and Windows limits (not usb-wdm-free)

- **2000's own `usbaudio.sys` on a MIDI function (AC + MS) stops 2000**
  (STOP 0x1E in `usbaudio.sys`), under both stacks; `usbmidi`'s release
  INF does not list the simulated AC+MS ids, so 2000 binds its own driver.
  The record is usb-wdm-free `a28c452` (`w47-midi2k`). With the TEST INF
  `usbmidi` binds and every MIDI profile passes on 2000.
- **2000 binds the whole composite X1 (`041E:3286`) to `HidUsb`** under its
  own hub driver, so `uac2` never gets its audio function there; under
  xHCI98 the composite parent splits it and `x1` passes.
- **8 and 8.1 x64 need a catalog** for `uac2`'s and `usbmidi`'s copy-only
  INFs (`pnputil -a`: "The third-party INF does not contain digital
  signature information").

### Rig artifacts

- **2000 and XP x64 under Windows' EHCI: a transfer descriptor run twice.**
  `ax88772` lost 1 to 5 of 20 fragmented pings (never at 1472 bytes);
  three pcap reruns showed 30 duplicated fragments, every one the same
  qTD (QEMU's packet id) with identical bytes, in order on the queue; no
  duplicate with a new packet id. Not a driver resubmission; xHCI98 shows
  none. TCP passed byte for byte.
- **Stock QEMU's isochronous path** (side checks on the fork, labelled
  `uac2fork`): XP's EHCI playback stops and xHCI98's synchronous and
  adaptive playback runs at about a third of real time; on the fork both
  play in real time.
- **The `x4` model refuses alternates it advertises** (it models the first
  X4 runs, IF4 alt 1 only). Vista and later then fail to open its output
  (their audio engine picks another format); XP and 2000 play it. A real
  X4 accepts every alternate it lists (`x4full` passes).
- **`cdcnet` on Vista x64**: QEMU's `usb-net` never appeared in Vista x64's
  device list on either stack (two runs), so `CDCRNDIS.INF` could not be
  installed by hand. Not settled.
- **`uac2` `bf92407` on 2000, `uac3dual`**: after Setup's restart and the cold
  start the guest never answered the test steps (timeouts, no screen
  fault); inconclusive, counted as FAIL in the table. `x1` on 2000 is the
  composite limit below.
- **`rtl815x` on Vista x64 under xHCI98**: the first plug, right after the
  by-hand install, found no adapter yet; the replug and restart passed.

### Earlier findings (`e6526a6`)

## The printer and libusb0 models

- `usbprn`'s legs use QEMU's `usb-printer` with the HP LaserJet 4 IEEE 1284
  id. Against Windows' own `usbprint` on XP: bound as "USB Printing
  Support", read GET_DEVICE_ID (98 bytes), created the LaserJet 4 child on
  USB001; a 109-byte RAW job arrived intact, and again after a replug; only
  GET_DEVICE_ID was requested. On 10 x64 `usbprint` read GET_DEVICE_ID 7
  times (98 of 98 bytes); the print job did not run (the guest's command
  channel failed). No mismatch with Windows' own driver was seen.
- `libusb0`'s records used QEMU's `usb-storage` (46F4:0001) through a test
  INF on 98 SE only; it was never checked against Windows' own driver.

Every set voided for a harness fault is in the kit's `legs-void\` with a

one. The faults: a stale simulator holding a port; 2000's warm restart
and Setup's restart prompts; the NT5 kernel audio stack installing after
the first audio device; a Logo dialog hidden behind the console and the

base build was "not valid for signing"); an unanswered publisher prompt on
Vista x86; the old-build reruns superseded by `bf92407`.
