# `rtl815x` in the 9x matrix: the harness's two fixes, and the Full Speed shapes again, 2026-11-09

Open items, "`rtl815x` in the 9x matrix: the harness, not the driver"
(owner, 2026-11-08). The 0.1.9 9x matrix's `rtl815x` FAILs (the guest sent
no frame) were the test setup: 98 SE binds TCP/IP to the first five
network adapters a guest makes (about nine on ME), one per device id,
root port and speed; and `rtlemu.py` did not join a bulk-OUT transfer
that QEMU's UHCI hands over one 64-byte packet at a time. Both are fixed
here, and the shapes that ran into the second were run again. All of this
is emulated (simulated chips); nothing here is a result on hardware.

## The fixes

- **`rtlemu.py`** (`ec8703a`): a bulk-OUT transfer that arrives in pieces
  is joined before it is parsed. A piece shorter than the endpoint's
  `wMaxPacketSize`, or a zero-length one, ends the transfer; a run of
  full-size pieces ends where its transmit records end with the data
  (their lengths reach the end, zero padding after the last allowed), so
  a transfer QEMU's EHCI or xHCI hands over whole is not held back. The
  first joined transfer is logged (`tx_joined`) and counted.
  `rtlsimtest.py` `test_bulk_out_joined_across_packets`: an RTL8152 and an
  RTL8157 transfer of three records in 64-byte pieces gives three frames
  once, a whole 128-byte transfer is sent at once, and a zero-length
  packet after it sends nothing.
- **The matrix harness** (`drivers.py` of the 9x matrix kit, not in this
  repository; the patched copy is in the evidence folder below): the
  shapes a leg runs are listed once (`shape_list`), each shape's adapter
  is keyed by device id, controller and speed (`adapter_key`), a leg makes
  at most five new adapters (`ADAPTERS_MAX`; a sixth shape is recorded
  SKIP, never run), and `drivers.py groups <driver> <os> <stack> <tag>`
  splits a driver's shapes, in order, into jobs of at most five new
  adapters each, one fresh overlay per job. For `rtl815x`: five jobs under
  98 SE's xHCI98 (16, 5, 8, 5 and 3 shapes), four under NUSB, five under
  ME's xHCI98, two under ME's own UHCI (the five Full Speed chips and
  ids, then the other three).
