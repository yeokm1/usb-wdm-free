# MTP M1: the driver's load and standby readings on 98 SE and ME, 2026-11-16 and 17

A load rerun of `USBMTP.SYS` after the changes of `e47c151`, `769ae01`
and `3044286`, above all the last one: the driver completes power IRPs
only at `PASSIVE_LEVEL` and is the device's power policy owner, so it
requests device power IRPs of its own (`PoRequestPowerIrp`) and imports
`KeGetCurrentThread` and `PoRequestPowerIrp`, neither of which had run on
a guest before. Three configurations, each with a standby leg: 98 SE
under xHCI98 3.5.0.0, 98 SE under NUSB 3.3's EHCI driver, ME under
xHCI98 3.5.0.0. Emulated only (QEMU's `usb-mtp`), headless; no real phone
or camera. A development reading, not a release and not "Tested".

The standby under NUSB's EHCI did not come back (below). On 2026-11-17
the hang was located with control runs and scratch trace builds, the
driver was changed to request no device power IRP (`4420af3`; the design
in [mtp.md](../design/mtp.md) 5.4, "Power", `678f4eb`), and all three
configurations were run again ("2026-11-17"). Later that day the
changed driver was read with data moving across a standby, on ME's kept
device after the wake, and with a second standby ("2026-11-17, later").
The standby rework, once in the driver (`0525a8e`), was read on all three
configurations, its refusal with a test-only build ("2026-11-17, the
standby rework in the driver"); a follow-up read what that refusal does
to 98 SE's serial ports and repeated, seven times (six with the handles
held across the sleep), the sequence of the one unexplained exit of the
emulator at a wake, which did not recur and stays unexplained
("2026-11-17, follow-up").

## Load and start

On all four guests the Add New Hardware Wizard, pointed at D:, installed
"MTP or PTP device (wdm-free)", and the trace showed
`driverentry 00010000`, `adddevice 1`, `cfgwalk 00000000 FF010027` (the
walk accepted; a whole device of one interface, 39 bytes) and
`start 00000000 00060200` (started; interface 0, class 06, bulk IN 512).
So the driver with its two new imports loaded on 98 SE (both stacks) and
on ME.

**`MTPTEST info`, `session`, `storage`** (on `x`, `e`, `m`; not on `e2`):
the same results on all three.

- `info`: interface 1.0, generation 1, `46F4:0004`, class 06/01/01,
  packets 512/512/64, speed 2, endpoints 81/02/83, the three strings.
- `session`: a second claim refused (32), the bind and timeout refusals
  (5), GetDeviceInfo 201 bytes in one read and its response, Get Device
  Status, waits timing out (121; the 500 ms wait took 508, 505 and 504
  ms), a pending read ended by `ABORT` (995 after 1,514, 1,505 and 1,507
  ms), a new claim after the close with the next epoch.
- `storage`: every response 2001h; GetDeviceInfo 189 of 189 bytes parsed;
  one storage, `00010001`, "SIMROOT"; 2 handles at its root.

## Standby, no handle open

Start, Shut Down, "Stand by", with nothing of the driver open; the
emulator then reported `paused (suspended)`; later the monitor's
`system_wakeup`.

**Into standby: the same trace on all four guests.**

```
power 00030000 00000004       QUERY_POWER, system, S3
power 00020000 00000004       SET_POWER, system, S3
power 00020001 00000004       SET_POWER, device, D3 (the driver's own request)
power-down 00000003 00000000  the D3 forwarded
d-done 00000001 00000000      its completion function: success
d-request 00000004 00000103   PoRequestPowerIrp(D3) returned STATUS_PENDING
power-down 00000001 00000001  the power worker forwards the S3 IRP
```

The D3 IRP reached the driver, was forwarded and completed, and its
completion function ran, all before `PoRequestPowerIrp` returned to the
system IRP's dispatch; the system IRP, kept pending, then went down from
the power worker.

**Out of standby under xHCI98 (`x`, 98 SE; `m`, ME): the same trace.**

```
power 00020000 00000001       SET_POWER, system, S0
power-up 00000002 00000000    S0 forwarded and completed: success
power 00020001 00000001       SET_POWER, device, D0 (the driver's own request)
power-up 00000004 00000000    D0 forwarded and completed: success
d-done 00000002 00000000      its completion function: success
d-request 00000001 00000103   PoRequestPowerIrp(D0) returned STATUS_PENDING
```

The device was not removed: `MTPTEST info` after the wake read the same
device, generation 1, and `session` and `storage` gave the results
above (the 500 ms wait 502 ms on both; the aborted read 1,504 and 1,505
ms; a claim's epochs 4 and 5).

**Out of standby under NUSB's EHCI (`e`, and again on `e2`): the guest
did not come back.** The same S0 and D0 lines as above, then, with no
`d-request` of the driver's before them:

```
power 00020001 00000003       SET_POWER, device, D2 (the driver requests only D3 and D0)
power-down 00000003 00000000  forwarded
power 00020001 00000001       SET_POWER, device, D0, a second one, not requested
power-up 00000004 00000000    forwarded and completed: success
remove 00000001 00000000      REMOVE, generation 1, no handle open
```

and nothing after it: no `remove-drain-waiting` (which the driver's wait
for its admissions logs once a second), no `remove-deletes` or
`deleting`. The screen still showed the Shut Down dialog from before the
standby, the clock did not move, and a key (Ctrl+Esc) changed nothing;
the emulator reported the guest running and its CPU not halted. `e` stayed so for
about 10 minutes; a `device_del` of the device then changed nothing more
in the trace. `e2`, with no test run before the standby, did the same,
line for line, and stayed so for about 10.5 minutes until it was ended. Where the system waits was not established. The driver's
own code between `remove` and `remove-deletes` has two waits without a
log line of their own (the lower driver's REMOVE, and the rundown loop
that waits for every slot to be retired), besides the logged one; with no
handle open and no slot in use none of them was expected to wait.

The earlier load run of this driver (`4360e64`, before the power policy
owner change), on the same base with the claim of the zombie test held
during the standby, had REMOVE arrive during the standby and the guest's
desktop back after the wake (the device not re-enumerated within 80 s).
That run is not in a tracked record; it is named here only for the
difference.

## 2026-11-17: where the standby hang stops, and the change

**Controls**, each on a fresh overlay of the NUSB base, `usb-mtp` on
`usb-ehci`, standby with no handle open, then the wake:

| Run | Driver | Into standby | After the wake |
|---|---|---|---|
| `o1`, `o2` | the earlier driver (trace `0503ea7e`, `4360e64`), which requests no device power IRP | `power` QUERY and SET S3, `power-down 0`, then `remove`, `remove-deletes`, `deleting`, `unload`: the device removed before the suspend | the desktop back, the clock running; the device not enumerated again |
| `n1`, `n2` | none (the wizard cancelled: an unknown device) | - | the desktop back, the clock running |

So this stack and this guest survive a standby by themselves, and with a
driver that sends no device power IRP.

**Scratch trace builds** of `3044286` with extra lines (not tracked; each
on its own fresh NUSB overlay, no handle open): every power and PnP IRP
at entry with its IRP and thread, the driver's own request with the IRP
`PoRequestPowerIrp` returned, the stack's capabilities, and REMOVE's steps
with times.

- **The stop point** (`d1`, `d2`, and every later run of the D3 design):
  REMOVE ran `Run`, drained its admissions (`rm-drained`: no requested
  D-IRP outstanding, no system IRP kept, the power worker ended), and then
  the trace ends: the next line, written when REMOVE's pass to the stack
  below returns, never came. The guest froze as before.
- **The order after the wake**, all on the same thread as the system
  IRPs: S0 forwarded and completed; the driver's D0 request, its IRP
  delivered, forwarded, completed, its completion function called, and
  the system IRP completed by the power worker; about 3.2 s later a D2
  (an IRP other than the driver's), forwarded; 0.4 s later a D0, forwarded
  and completed; 0.36 s later REMOVE.
- **The stack's capabilities** (`c1`): S0 to D0, S1 to S5 to D3, device
  wake D2, D1 and D2 supported, wake from D0 to D2. The unrequested D2
  has the value of the device wake state the stack reports; why its
  sender sent it is not established.
- **Variants**, one run each:

| Run | Change from `3044286` | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `c2` | no D3 requested at S3 (D0 after S0 kept) | QUERY_REMOVE and REMOVE as the system went to sleep, REMOVE's pass returned in 14 ms; the desktop back after the wake |
| `x1` | D3 at S3, D0 after S0; the unrequested D2 completed by the driver, not passed down | the same stop: after `rm-drained` nothing |
| `x3` | D3 at S3, no D0 requested after S0 | frozen earlier: after S0 completed no further IRP reached the driver |

None of the three candidates set for the investigation is what the trace
shows: the driver's D0 request had completed long before REMOVE, no power
IRP or completion of the driver's was outstanding when REMOVE came, and
REMOVE's own drain had finished. What the runs separate is the D3: with a
D3 passed down for the standby this stack does not come back; without
one it removes the device as the system goes to sleep and comes back.
The stack's code was not read; where inside it REMOVE waits is not known.

**The change.** The driver no longer requests device power IRPs
(mtp.md 5.4, "Power"): a system sleep closes the gate and goes down once
quiet, S0 reopens it; a device IRP another component sends is handled as
before. `slotmodel.c` states the stack's behaviour as read and catches
the design of `3044286` as a mutant.

## What this shows and what it does not

It shows `USBMTP.SYS` of `3044286` loading and starting on 98 SE under
both stacks and on ME under xHCI98, with its interface 1.0 answering as
before, and the power policy owner's path running on all three: the D3
request at S3 and the D0 request after S0, each D-IRP delivered and its
completion function called once, and the system IRP finished from the
power worker. `power-late` did not appear in any run: no power IRP came
to the driver above `PASSIVE_LEVEL`.

It also showed a defect of that design: on 98 SE under NUSB's EHCI a
standby with the device present and idle ended in a guest that did not
come back from the standby, in every run of it (two load runs and the
scratch runs `d1`, `d2`, `c1`, `x1`, `x3`). In all of them but `x3`
the trace ends after REMOVE drained its admissions: REMOVE's pass to the
stack below never returned. `x3` (no D0 requested) froze earlier, with no
IRP after S0, so it shares the failure to resume, not the place. The
driver of `4420af3`, which requests no device power IRP, came back from
the standby on all three configurations (one run each), and read the
power gate's code, 21.

With `4420af3`'s sources and data moving (the section before): on ME the
kept device carried a transfer across a standby (`mb`; on `ma` the
earlier test form opened no new session and read 2003), with a new
session after the wake moving correct data, and `session` and `storage`
passed after the wake on both; on 98 SE under xHCI98 the device was
removed at each of two standbys in one guest and the new device worked
after each; under NUSB's EHCI four standbys during a transfer went
through, the device was not enumerated again (nor a replugged one, also
without this driver), and one standby hung in this driver's wait for
quiet, a cancelled request never returned. These are emulated readings
on QEMU's `usb-mtp`; the data moved was bulk IN, with only command
containers going out.
