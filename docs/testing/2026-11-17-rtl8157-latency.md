# rtl815x and interactive latency: the RTL8157 on simulated legs, 2026-11-17

A question of interactive latency (a remote desktop over an RTL8157 felt
slow): small frames at a low rate, both directions. The records so far
counted ping replies but never measured round-trip time. This record
reads the data path against Linux and measures what the simulator can
show. Every leg ran against `rtlemu.py` (simulated chip); no physical
adapter was used and none is claimed. The simulator hands each frame to
the next bulk-IN request at once: it has none of the real chip's
aggregation timers, so these legs show the driver's and the emulated
host controller's delay only.

## The data path, as read

Source at `456b56b` (the `rtl815x` code of 0.2.2.0 and 0.2.3.0 is the
same; 0.2.3.0 changed only the INFs and version).

- **Receive aggregation (chip).** On the RTL8157 and RTL8159 the link-up
  steps (`SqRxOnMG`, `rtlchip.c` lines 1089-1111) clear
  `UR_RX_AGG_NUM` bits 0-8 (no frame limit), write `UR_RX_EARLY_TMO`
  640/8 (8 ns units: 640 ns) and `UR_PM_STATUS` (0xd432, the extra
  aggregation timer) 15000/8 (8 ns units: 15 us), then the early size
  (`H_RX_EARLY`, line 1915) in 16-byte units: (32768 - 1522 - 16 - 16) /
  16. Linux `r8152.c` (v7.3, the file of `f47d2236`; facts only, the file
  is GPL-2.0-only) writes the same for `RTL_VER_16` and `RTL_VER_17`:
  `rtl8156_enable` clears `RX_AGGR_NUM_MASK` in `USB_RX_AGGR_NUM`;
  `r8153_set_rx_early_timeout` writes `USB_RX_EARLY_TIMEOUT` 640/8 and
  `USB_RX_EXTRA_AGGR_TMR` `coalesce`/8, with `coalesce` 15000 ns from
  `r8156_init` at every USB and link speed (the `COALESCE_SUPER`,
  `COALESCE_HIGH` and `COALESCE_SLOW` choice by USB speed is the
  RTL8153's, in `r8153_init`); `r8153_set_rx_early_size` writes
  (`rx_buf_sz` - `rx_reserved_size`) / 16, `rx_buf_sz` 32 KiB on the
  RTL8157 and 48 KiB on the RTL8159 (here 32 KiB for both: a smaller
  early size, never a later delivery). Linux sets no other receive
  aggregation timer on these chips. Linux lets `ethtool -C rx-usecs`
  change `coalesce`; this driver has no such setting.

| Register (chip) | rtl815x RTL8157/8159 | Linux RTL_VER_16/17 | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `USB_RX_EARLY_TIMEOUT` 0xd42c | 80 (640 ns), every speed | 80 (640 ns), every speed | 8 ns |
| `USB_RX_EXTRA_AGGR_TMR` 0xd432 | 1875 (15 us), every speed | 1875 (15 us, `coalesce` 15000), every speed | 8 ns |
| `USB_RX_EARLY_SIZE` 0xd42e | (32768 - 1554) / 16 = 1950 | RTL8157: 1950; RTL8159: (49152 - 1554) / 16 = 2974 | 16 bytes |
| `USB_RX_AGGR_NUM` 0xd4ee bits 0-8 | cleared | cleared | frames |
| For comparison, RTL8153 | 85 / 250 / 524.28 us by USB speed | the same (`COALESCE_*`) | 8 ns |

- **Receive (driver).** Eight bulk-IN transfers of 32 KiB are kept
  outstanding (`rtlchip.c` line 2047, `netusb.c` `RX_MAX`), each reposted
  through the pump as its completion returns. On 2000 and later (and in
  the x64 build) the completion routine parses the transfer and
  indicates its frames to NDIS at once, up to 16 per call
  (`netusb.c` `Done` lines 586-681, `netcore.c` `Receive` lines
  323-423). On 98 SE and ME the frames are queued and indicated by an
  NDIS timer set with no delay (`netcore.c` `ArmUpcalls` lines 141-150,
  `Upcalls` lines 186-224), as section 4.4 of the design describes.
- **Transmit.** `SendPackets` queues and calls the pump directly
  (`netcore.c` lines 896-937, `netusb.c` `NetUsbKick` line 1388); the
  pump fills the first free of four transmit slots with everything
  queued, by bytes up to 32 KiB, and submits it at once (`BuildTx`, lines
  452-525). Nothing waits for a timer or for more frames: a lone frame
  goes in its own transfer, a burst shares transfers, and a frame waits
  only when all four slots are on the bus.
- **No sleep or poll on the data path.** The worker's 2 s link poll is a
  control read beside the data pipes; only a link change (or a failed
  transfer) quiesces them (`netusb.c` lines 1030-1086).
- **Power features on the RTL8157 by default:** EEE, green Ethernet and
  EEE+ off (the Advanced tab's defaults); the chip's U1/U2 off while the
  link is up and U2P3 off, as Linux; UPS and power cut off; ALDPS, EEE and
  10M clock division and MAC clock slow-down on, as Linux; no selective
  suspend (NDIS 5).
- **Offload defaults:** the transmit checksums (IP, TCP, UDP) are offered
  on 2000 and later and on by default; receive checksums and the large
  send are not offered (section 4.9). A frame carrying an offload is left
  unpadded for the chip to pad (`netusb.c` line 492). 98 SE and ME have
  no offload.

## Rig

- QEMU 11.1.0 (scoop build), TCG, headless; at most two guests at once.
- XP SP3 (overlay of `vm/wxp.qcow2`, pc machine, 2 vCPUs, 512 MiB):
  Windows' own EHCI, the RTL8157 at High Speed; later xHCI98 3.5.0.0 as
  released (`INSTALL.BAT /console /yes /nopause`, exit 3, restart) on
  `qemu-xhci`, the RTL8157 at SuperSpeed (5000 Mb/s link).
- 98 SE (overlay of `vm/w98se.qcow2`, NUSB 3.3 EHCI, 1 vCPU, 256 MiB):
  the RTL8157 at High Speed.
- Control: the same guest's emulated PCI adapter on the same peer - XP's
  in-box RTL8139 driver (`rtl8139`), 98 SE's in-box RTL8029 driver
  (`ne2k_pci`), each on its own subnet.
- `rtl815x` as released in 0.1.11 (`RTL815X.SYS` x86 SHA-256
  `f7e7c9d0...6edb`), `rtlemu.py --chip 8157 --udp` at `456b56b`.
- The peer: a scratch script (not tracked) on the other side of both UDP
  links (QEMU's dgram framing): it answers ARP, DHCP and ICMP echo, runs
  a TCP echo service on port 23, and times on the host clock the pings
  it sends to the guest and the guest's TCP acknowledgements. Each round
  trip is peer -> UDP -> rtlemu -> usb-redir -> emulated controller ->
  guest -> back.

- The echo -> ACK times are the guests' delayed acknowledgement (an echo
  is acknowledged at the stack's delayed-ACK timer, up to about 200 ms),
  the same on both paths. Every segment was acknowledged, none out of
  order. The guest's keystroke segments arrived spaced as they were
  typed (median gap 341-342 ms on every path; on XP 322-363 ms through
  rtl815x and 185-500 ms through the RTL8139, on 98 SE 338-345 ms on
  both): no transmit was held back.
- The single long acknowledgements on XP's rtl815x path (202 and 312 ms)
  were each the last, odd segment of a stream or burst, waiting for the
  delayed-ACK timer; the 312 ms one exceeds 200 ms and was not repeated
  or explained.
- The added round trip over the control adapter is about 2.6 ms
  (median) through the emulated EHCI, 0.75 ms through xHCI98 on the
  emulated xHCI, and 3.6 ms on 98 SE (whose receive indications go
  through the NDIS timer). It is the emulated USB path (QEMU's
  controller model, usb-redir, the simulator) plus the driver; the legs
  cannot split the two, and no per-frame trace exists in the driver
  (the trace build has no time stamps and no per-frame line).

## Limits

- Simulated chip, emulated host controllers, TCG guests: the numbers are
  relative, and none is a real machine's.
- The real RTL8157's aggregation timers (640 ns and 15 us, read above)
  and its USB 3 link behaviour are not modelled.
- QEMU 11.1's `qemu-xhci` and `nec-usb-xhci` both report HCIVERSION 1.0
  (`hw/usb/hcd-xhci.c`), so a controller below xHCI 1.0 cannot be
  emulated with them.
- No Vista or 7 x64 latency leg (the x64 build has no 98 deferral, so
  its data path is the NT one measured on XP).
